## Answer

A policy says what must happen and why. A procedure says how it happens, step by step.

**Policy**
- States the organization's rules and position, such as "We do not offer, give or accept bribes."
- Sets scope, who it covers, required and prohibited conduct, accountability and consequences of breach.
- Approved at a senior level (often the Board or a Board committee) and changed rarely.
- Kept stable and independent of particular systems or teams.

**Procedure**
- Turns the policy into executable steps: who does what, in what order, in which system, by when and what record each step leaves.
- Covers exceptions, escalation and the evidence produced.
- Owned by the operating function and updated whenever the process or system changes.
- Specific enough that a new employee can follow it and an auditor can test it.

**A quick test:** if a line states an outcome the organization requires, it belongs in the policy. If it says who approves, clicks, files or checks something and when, it belongs in a procedure.

**Why the distinction matters**
- A policy without procedures is aspiration. Regulators and auditors look at how the rule operates in practice, and issuing a policy does not show it was adopted.
- Mixing them makes policies brittle (every process tweak needs Board approval) and procedures vague.
- Two related layers often sit between them: standards set mandatory specifics (for example, minimum password length) and guidelines give recommended but optional practice.
