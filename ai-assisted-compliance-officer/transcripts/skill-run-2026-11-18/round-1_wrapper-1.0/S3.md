## Escalation statement

**Known facts (all user-stated, none verified by me):**
- An engineer reported a safety violation to a regulator on 2026-11-17 (yesterday, Asia/Seoul).
- Her manager then told her she will be "managed out".
- She emailed HR about this about an hour ago. You are the compliance officer and now know about it.

**Unknowns:** which jurisdiction(s) and regulator are involved; whether the safety violation is still going on and is putting people at risk; whether any adverse step has already been taken (performance plan, reassignment, lost access, a termination date); the manager's seniority and reporting line; whether the threat is in writing or has witnesses; whether she has a lawyer; whether the regulator has contacted the company.

**Urgency: high.** If the facts hold, this is a credible threat of retaliation against a protected reporter, made the day after her report. It raises three separate problems: possible ongoing harm from the safety issue, possible retaliation and the risk of losing evidence. The closer an adverse action comes to the report, the harder it is to defend. Some regimes presume retaliation in this situation (see the law section below).

**Recommended recipient and channel:** today, in writing, to the General Counsel. Ask the GC to engage ⚠ **external employment counsel**, because a regulator is involved and there may be statutory penalty exposure. If the manager sits in the GC's line, or senior management is implicated, or the GC has a personal interest, take it instead to the chair of the audit (or ethics) committee of the Board.

**Deadlines:** none verified. The law of the jurisdiction decides whether any company-side deadline applies. Her own deadlines to file a retaliation complaint with an authority are hers. The company must not take any step meant to make her miss them.

**Protective steps (each needs a human decision; I take none of them):**
1. **Freeze adverse action.** Recommend in writing that no employment decision about her is taken while the matter is reviewed. That includes performance ratings, a performance plan, reassignment, cutting her hours, removing access or exit talks. Pay, role, access and duties stay as they are unless she asks for a change.
2. **Remove the manager from decisions about her.** Put an interim decision-maker in place who is independent of him. Do not move her away from him in a way that looks like a demotion or isolation. Any change of reporting line should be one she accepts.
3. **Instruct the manager in writing, through counsel or HR.** Tell him to take no action against her, not to discuss her report with colleagues, and not to try to find out or comment on what she told the regulator. Tell him to preserve his communications.
4. **Preservation hold today.** Cover her email to HR, the manager's emails, chats, texts and calendar entries, her performance and HR file as it stands now, and all records of the underlying safety issue. Suspend auto-deletion for the custodians involved. Take a dated snapshot of her HR file so no later "performance" record can be backdated.
5. **Triage the safety issue on its own track.** Have the responsible operations or safety owner check today whether the reported condition creates ongoing risk to people, and contain it if it does. Do this whatever happens to the retaliation question.
6. **Respond to her the same day, through HR or counsel.** Acknowledge her email. Confirm the company prohibits retaliation and is reviewing her concern. Tell her plainly that she is free to talk to the regulator and to her own lawyer. Give her a contact independent of the manager. Tell her honestly what confidentiality the process can and cannot give.

**Decision needed from you, today:** will you escalate to the GC (or to the audit committee chair if the line is compromised) and recommend steps 1 to 6 now, ahead of any investigation finding? My recommendation is yes.

## TL;DR
- Treat this as a protected-reporting retaliation risk now. Do not wait to find out whether the safety report was accurate: in most regimes, protection does not depend on that.
- Before anyone decides anything else, freeze adverse action, take the manager out of decisions about her and put a preservation hold in place.
- Run two tracks: the safety issue (harm and containment) and the retaliation complaint (an independent investigation). Keep them apart.
- Nobody in the company should ask her what she told the regulator, discourage her, or ask her to withdraw her report or a complaint.
- ⧉ The single biggest risk is a "performance" or restructuring action against her in the coming weeks that is documented after the fact. It would look like retaliation and could become a separate violation in its own right.

## Law: provisional; jurisdiction not given
I have not assumed a jurisdiction. Here are three likely regimes, as examples:
- **Korea.** The [Act on the Protection of Public Interest Whistleblowers](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/kor/act-on-the-protection-of-public-interest-whistleblowers_html/Republic_of_Korea_Act_on_the_Protection_of_Public_Interest_Whistleblowers.pdf) is relevant. I opened it in the UNODC-hosted English translation of the original Act No. 10472 of 2011-03-29, which is out of date. In that text, Article 15(1) prohibits disadvantageous measures taken in retaliation for whistleblowing. Article 17(2) gives the reporter three months to ask the Commission for protective measures. Article 23 presumes retaliation where someone tries to obstruct a report or force its withdrawal, or where a disadvantageous measure follows within two years of the report. The current version is [Act No. 20385 of 2024-03-19 on KLRI](https://elaw.klri.re.kr/eng_service/lawViewTitle.do?hseq=69285). I could see only its article titles there, which still include Article 15 "Prohibition of Disadvantageous Measures" and Article 23 "Presumption of Disadvantageous Measures". I have not verified the current wording, time limits or penalties. My recollection, which is not verified, is that the three-month period was later extended, and that the Occupational Safety and Health Act separately protects workers who report to the Ministry of Employment and Labor. Check both against law.go.kr.
- **United States.** OSHA [says](https://www.osha.gov/workers/file-complaint) that workers may file a whistleblower complaint if they believe their employer retaliated against them, and that filing deadlines run from 30 to 180 days depending on the statute. I could not open the OSH Act Section 11(c) page, so the deadline that applies here is not verified. Sector statutes (transportation, nuclear, energy, environmental) may also apply. So may state law.
- **EU.** Recalled and unverified (my training cutoff is June 2026): Directive (EU) 2019/1937 prohibits retaliation against reporters, including threats of retaliation. It reverses the burden of proof once the reporter shows a report and a detriment. Coverage depends on the Member State's implementing law.

Not finding a rule does not mean none applies. Counsel should confirm the controlling law once the jurisdiction is known.
