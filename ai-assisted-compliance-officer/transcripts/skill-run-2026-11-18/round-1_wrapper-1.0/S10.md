## 1. Why I did not write "the control failed"

**Issue.** Does the absence of 2024 training records support a finding that the anti-bribery training control failed?

**Rule.** Under the Compliance Officer prompt (section 5), missing evidence means unverified, not automatically noncompliant. "Evidence unavailable" must also be kept separate from "evidence contradicting a claim." Audit testing works the same way: a control whose performance cannot be evidenced fails the operating-effectiveness test, but that is a different conclusion from "the activity did not occur." Regulators look at both delivery and proof. The [DOJ Criminal Division's *Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs* (updated September 2024)](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/page/file/937501/dl) asks what training employees in relevant control functions have received, whether high-risk and control employees received tailored training, and how the company has measured the training's effectiveness. A company that cannot show records will struggle to answer any of those questions.

**Application.** You have stated one fact: there are no 2024 records. Unknown: whether the training was scheduled, delivered or recorded somewhere else, and which systems and entities were searched. A finding that says "failed" would assert more than the evidence supports. It would also be the first thing management challenges.

**Conclusion.** Report a test exception: operation not evidenced, effectiveness not concluded. Assess recordkeeping separately. Upgrade to "not delivered" only on positive evidence.

## 2. Before you issue: search-completeness check

Confirm each of these and record the result in the workpaper. Each one can change the finding.

1. **Systems searched:** the HR LMS, any vendor e-learning platform, regional HR, legal/compliance shared drives, email invitations, calendar entries and paper attendance sheets.
2. **Entities searched:** every in-scope subsidiary and branch, especially those in higher-risk jurisdictions.
3. **Requests made:** a written request to the control owner, with the date and the response (or the lack of one). Do not rely on a verbal "we have nothing."
4. **Design check:** what the control actually requires: frequency (annual?), population (all staff or high-risk roles?) and evidence (completion log, quiz score, attestation). If the design never required records, the finding is a design gap, not an operating failure.
5. **Corroboration either way:** a vendor invoice, training materials dated 2024, or a statement from the owner that no session was held.

**Integrity note.** If records are now reconstructed or collected late, they must carry their true creation date and author, labeled as reconstructed. Do not accept or file backdated completion logs or attestations.
