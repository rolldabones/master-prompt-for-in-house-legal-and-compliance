## Part 1: The fictional policy rules the register applies

> **FICTIONAL TRAINING SAMPLE.** Saltmarsh Valve Works Ltd., its people, counterparties and thresholds are invented. Do not use these figures as legal limits.

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| R1 | Gifts or hospitality received up to USD 50 a person: record within 5 business days, no approval needed |
| R2 | USD 50.01 to USD 250 a person: line manager approval **before** accepting or giving |
| R3 | Over USD 250 a person, **or** anything from or to a party in an active tender or negotiation, **or** anything to a public official: Compliance pre-approval in every case |
| R4 | Cash and cash equivalents (gift cards, vouchers, securities) are never accepted or given, whatever the amount |
| R5 | Hospitality needs a genuine business purpose and the host present. If the host does not attend, the offer counts as a gift |
| R6 | Travel and accommodation offered by a third party: Compliance pre-approval. The company normally pays its own travel |
| R7 | Every offer goes in the register, including offers that were declined |

## Part 3: Reviewer notes by entry

**GH-2026-001: Low value, logged only.** Modest branded item, no open tender, logged the next day. *Teaching point:* low-value items still go in the register (R1, R7).

**GH-2026-002: Hospitality given, approved in advance.** Approval came 4 days before the dinner, the host attended and the purpose is documented. *Teaching point:* approval has to come before the event. The per-head figure is the one that counts against R2.

**GH-2026-003: Approved with conditions.** The full offer of USD 1,400 went over R3 and included travel under R6. Compliance approved only the conference pass and had Saltmarsh pay its own travel. *Teaching point:* the choice is not just accept or decline. Narrowing the offer keeps the business benefit and removes the risk of looking obligated. Both the offered value and the accepted value are recorded.

**GH-2026-004: Should have been declined.** Six red flags:
1. **Active tender:** Ferrogate is bidding in RFP-2026-017.
2. **Decision-maker:** the recipient evaluates that tender.
3. **Value:** USD 950 is well over the R3 threshold.
4. **No host:** Ferrogate did not attend, so under R5 this is a gift, not hospitality.
5. **Accommodation:** the hotel night brings in R6.
6. **No pre-approval, and logged 19 days late.**

Recorded remediation, all proposed and none yet completed:
- REM-2026-014: the employee reimburses Ferrogate at face value, with evidence of payment kept.
- REM-2026-015: the Head of Procurement decides whether the employee is recused from RFP-2026-017. A second evaluator re-reviews the scoring.
- REM-2026-016: refresher training for the procurement team.

Any disciplinary outcome is a decision for HR and management, not for the register. *Teaching point:* the entry records what actually happened, including the breach and the late logging. Nobody back-dates it or rewrites it to "declined." An accurate record of a breach is what makes remediation possible.

**GH-2026-005: Properly declined.** A gift card is a cash equivalent, so R4 bans it at any value, even though USD 200 would fall within R2. It was returned with a standard letter and the decline was logged under R7. *Teaching point:* set this beside GH-2026-004. A smaller offer was handled correctly and recorded.

## Part 4: Facilitator materials

**Knowledge check (answers in brackets):**
1. Which entry needed no approval at all? [GH-2026-001]
2. Why is GH-2026-005 declined when USD 200 would pass R2? [It is a cash equivalent; R4 applies at any value]
3. Name three separate reasons GH-2026-004 should have been declined. [Any three of the six red flags above]
4. In GH-2026-003, why record both 1,400 and 450? [The offered value shows the risk; the accepted value shows what was actually received]
5. A trainee suggests changing GH-2026-004 to "declined" now that the money has been repaid. What is wrong with that? [It makes the record false. The correct record is the breach plus the remediation]

**Optional discussion extension:** change GH-2026-002 so the guests are officials from a state-owned utility. Which rule now applies, and what changes? [R3: Compliance pre-approval. Rules for public officials are usually stricter and vary by country]
