**IRAC on the main 72-hour rule (GDPR)**

- **Issue:** When must a controller notify the authority after a laptop holding personal data is stolen?
- **Rule:** <cite>"In the case of a personal data breach, the controller shall without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it, notify ... the supervisory authority"</cite>, <cite>"unless the personal data breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons"</cite> ([GDPR Art. 33, gdpr-info.eu](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/), an unofficial consolidation).
- **Application:** Learning of the theft is very likely "awareness". Stolen customer records point to a risk to customers unless the disk was strongly encrypted and the key was not exposed. That is the strongest contrary argument and could support a documented "unlikely to result in a risk" decision. Even then, you should record the decision.
- **Conclusion:** If GDPR applies and the device was not encrypted, the deadline was 2026-11-16 at about 18:00 KST. Notify now and give reasons for the delay.

**Korea notes**

The amendment in force from 2026-09-11 widened what counts as a breach. It also added a duty to notify individuals within 72 hours of learning of a *possible* breach (Decree Art. 39-2), and that notice is replaced if the breach is confirmed ([Law Times](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=226491)). Decree Art. 40 sets 72 hours for the report to PIPC or KISA. The report is required for 1,000+ people affected, for sensitive or unique-identifier data, or for unlawful external access ([itwiki, Decree Art. 40](https://itwiki.kr/w/개인정보_보호법_시행령_제40조), a secondary source. I could not open the official text on [law.go.kr](https://law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lspttninfSeq=67073)). See also [PIPC](https://pipc.go.kr/np/default/page.do?mCode=D030040000) and [Daeryun newsletter](https://www.daeryunlaw.com/newsletter/news/250). ⚠ If it is a PIPC matter, use external counsel.
