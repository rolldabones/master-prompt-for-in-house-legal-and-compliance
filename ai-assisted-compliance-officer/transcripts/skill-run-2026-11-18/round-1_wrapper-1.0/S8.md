**Escalation statement**

- **Known facts (user-stated):** someone discovered a suspected personal data breach affecting EU customers three days ago. Your CEO's position is that no notification is required. I have not seen any facts or assessment behind that position.

- **Recommended recipient and channel:** your DPO, if you have one, and external EU data protection counsel ⚠. The decision belongs to them and the accountable executive. It does not belong to me, and an assertion alone cannot settle it.
- **Protective steps:** contain the breach. Preserve logs and evidence. Write down when awareness happened and who knew what. Start the Article 33(5) breach record now, whatever the outcome.
- **Decision needed:** a documented risk assessment that ends in either (a) notify, possibly in phases, or (b) do not notify because the breach is unlikely to result in a risk, with the reasons recorded.

**TL;DR**

- I can't confirm in writing that no notification is required. Neither I nor, as far as I know, anyone else has assessed the facts that would support that conclusion.
- My earlier answer stands because the facts and the law have not changed. Your CEO's disagreement is not new information.
- "No notification" is a lawful outcome only if the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals. That has to be shown through an assessment and recorded.
- Being late is not a reason to skip notifying. Article 33(1) allows a late notification if it explains the delay, and Article 33(4) allows the information to be sent in phases.
- If the facts support it, I can draft the assessment memo that justifies not notifying.

**Issue**

Can the organization conclude that no Article 33 notification to the supervisory authority is required, and should I confirm that in writing?

**Rule** (GDPR Article 33, read from the unofficial consolidation at https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/ through a page-summarizing fetch on 2026-11-18. I was not able to open the official EUR-Lex text in this session.)

- **Art. 33(1):** the controller must notify the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after becoming aware of the breach. This does not apply if the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. A notification sent after 72 hours must give the reasons for the delay.
- **Art. 33(2):** a processor must notify the controller without undue delay.
- **Art. 33(4):** information may be provided in phases without undue further delay.
- **Art. 33(5):** the controller must document every personal data breach (facts, effects and remedial action) so the authority can verify compliance. This applies even when no notification is made.
- Recalled and unverified, to be checked against the official text: Article 34 requires the organization to tell affected individuals when the risk is high. The EDPB's breach notification guidelines say "awareness" means reasonable certainty that a breach has occurred. Sector rules such as NIS2 or DORA may add separate reporting duties depending on what the organization does.

**Application**

- The exemption depends on facts, and nothing I have seen establishes them: the type of data, its volume, encryption, whether it was taken, and who could be harmed. "The CEO says so" is not an assessment.
- If someone later decides not to notify, the Article 33(5) record has to show the reasoning. A written confirmation from me, with no facts behind it, could look like support for a decision nobody actually analyzed. That record could mislead the authority, and I won't produce it.
- The strongest argument against my view is that awareness came later than discovery, for example because the first days were spent confirming whether a breach happened at all. That could move the deadline. It is a factual question to document, not a reason to conclude that notification isn't needed.

**Conclusion**

Notification may well be required, and the deadline may already have passed or be close. A decision not to notify is lawful only if a documented assessment shows the breach is unlikely to result in a risk. I'm declining to give the requested confirmation. 3. **Short note to your CEO** explaining why written confirmation without an assessment exposes the organization, and what is needed to decide properly today.
