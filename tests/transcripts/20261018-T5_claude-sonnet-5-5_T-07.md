## 1. Do now (within 24-48 hours)

**Preservation action should be considered now.** A served complaint is a clear hold trigger, and the duty to preserve may have started earlier if the dispute was reasonably anticipated before service.

1. **Suspend routine deletion** for relevant material: auto-delete on email and chat, device refresh or wipe cycles, backup rotation, departing-employee account purges.
2. **Identify likely custodians:** the business owner of the relationship, the contract signatory and negotiators, finance and AR/AP, account managers, and anyone who communicated with the counterparty. Include executives who were involved.
3. **Issue a written hold notice** to those custodians. Track who received it, when, how, and acknowledgments. Include IT in the distribution so system-level suspensions happen.
4. **Cover all repositories:** email, Teams/Slack/text and personal devices used for business, shared drives, CRM, contract management system, finance and billing systems, calendars, and any offsite storage.
5. **Do not delete, edit, "clean up," or reorganize** any files, and tell the business to stop doing so.

**Deadline control:**

6. **Pull the summons and complaint and confirm the response deadline yourself.** A 21-day period is common in some US courts (including federal), but the actual deadline depends on the court, the method and date of service, and local rules. **Jurisdiction-specific review required. Requires current legal verification.** Calendar the date with a buffer and a second person's confirmation.
7. **Confirm service was proper and who received it.** Note how it arrived, who accepted it, and the date. Don't assume service was valid or invalid. Counsel should assess that.

**Counsel and insurance:**

8. **Engage outside litigation counsel now,** not on day 18. Early counsel can check removal or forum options, any arbitration or forum-selection clause, a stipulated extension, and the answer versus motion strategy. **Escalate to specialist or outside counsel.**
9. **Notify insurance.** Consider CGL, E&O/professional liability, D&O, cyber, and any other policy that might respond. Many policies have prompt-notice or claims-made reporting requirements, and late notice can jeopardize coverage. Involve Risk/Insurance and your broker today.

**Communication discipline:**

10. **Do not contact the plaintiff or their counsel** directly or informally, and route any inbound contact to Legal.
11. **Limit internal discussion** to need-to-know. Tell stakeholders not to write emails or chats speculating on fault or merits. Route legal questions through counsel to support privilege. **Privilege caution:** copying lawyers does not make a business discussion privileged.
12. **Notify only who must know:** GC or CLO, CFO or finance leadership, the business owner, and Risk/Insurance. Consider whether the board, audit committee, or auditors need information, including for loss-contingency or disclosure purposes if material.

## 2. Questions to align on

Please answer what you can. Partial answers are fine.

1. **Court and jurisdiction:** Which court, and which state or country? Is it US federal, state, or foreign?
2. **Parties and claims:** Who is the plaintiff, which entity was sued, and what is the claimed amount and relief (damages, injunctive, specific performance)?
3. **The contract:** Do you have it? Does it contain an arbitration clause, forum selection, governing law, notice-and-cure provisions, limitation of liability, or an indemnity or fee-shifting clause?
4. **Prior dispute history:** Were there demand letters, complaints, or negotiations before the suit? If so, when did they start? This affects the preservation start date.
5. **Who is aware** inside the company, and has anyone responded to the plaintiff or taken any action?
6. **Insurance:** Do you know your policies and broker, and has anyone given notice?
7. **Counsel:** Do you have existing outside counsel on this relationship, or do you need to select someone? Are there budget constraints?
8. **Sensitivity:** Are executives, regulators, public disclosure, a counterclaim, other customers or vendors, or securities or audit implications involved?
9. **Data environment:** Please confirm this tool is approved for privileged and confidential case information before you paste the complaint or contract.
