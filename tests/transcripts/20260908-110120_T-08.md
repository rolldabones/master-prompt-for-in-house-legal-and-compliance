# T-08 Outside Counsel Management

**Model:** claude-opus-5  **Run:** 20260908-110120

## User message

We're retaining a new firm for IP litigation. Help me set up outside counsel guidelines, budget controls and how we'll evaluate them.

## Assistant response

**Mode: OUTSIDE COUNSEL MANAGEMENT** (likely sequencing into LITIGATION RESPONSE and POLICY/PROCESS DESIGN)

Before I draft, two flags and one alignment step.

---

## Immediate flags (act on these independently of the OCG work)

**1. Preservation.** If IP litigation is filed, threatened or reasonably anticipated, the litigation hold obligation has likely already attached — it does not wait for the firm to be retained. **Preservation action should be considered now.** Tell me the posture and I can produce custodian scoping and a hold notice separately.

**2. Insurance notice.** Some IP exposure is picked up under advertising injury coverage, IP riders, or a supplier/customer indemnity. Late notice forfeits coverage. Confirm whether notice has been evaluated. Also confirm whether any third party owes you defense or indemnity — that changes who controls counsel selection and who approves the budget.

---

## Clarifying questions

Answer what you can; mark the rest unknown and I'll proceed with stated assumptions.

**Matter posture**
1. Plaintiff or defendant? Patent, trade secret, trademark, copyright, or mixed?
2. Filed or pre-suit? Venue(s)? Any parallel PTAB/IPR, ITC, or foreign proceedings?
3. Rough exposure or claim value, and is this bet-the-company, material, or routine?

**Firm and retention**
4. Firm already selected, or still in selection? If selected, has a conflicts check cleared — including adversity to affiliates, and any patent **prosecution bar** implications for the firm's other work?
5. Whose paper governs — their engagement letter, your OCG, or both? Your OCG should control on conflict, with an express order-of-precedence clause.

**Fee structure and budget**
6. Hourly, blended rate, phased fixed fee, capped, contingency, or hybrid? IP litigation is unusually amenable to phase-based fixed fees and hybrid arrangements.
7. What is the budget envelope and who holds authority to approve overruns?

**Internal ownership**
8. Who is the day-to-day supervising attorney? Who approves significant litigation actions (dispositive motions, expert retention, settlement positions)? What are the dollar thresholds in your approval matrix?

**Systems**
9. Do you have an e-billing/matter management platform, and do you require LEDES/UTBMS coded invoices with litigation phase codes? Without phase coding, budget-to-actual tracking will not work.
10. Is there an existing company-wide OCG this must sit inside, or is this greenfield?

**Deliverable**
11. Do you want one integrated document, or three separable artifacts (Guidelines / Budget & Billing Annex / Evaluation Scorecard)? Any deadline?

## Proposed deliverable architecture

So you can see what you'd be approving. Say "proceed" and I'll draft, filling gaps with labeled assumptions.

**Artifact 1 — Outside Counsel Guidelines**
- Scope, precedence over firm engagement letter, amendment control
- Relationship partner; named staffing roster; **no staffing changes without prior written approval**; no unapproved timekeepers
- Advance-approval matrix: pleadings, dispositive and significant motions, expert retention, discovery scope decisions, third-party subpoenas, settlement communications, any public filing or statement
- Prohibition on independent settlement or media contact
- Reporting cadence: case assessment memo at day 30–45, monthly status, immediate notice of adverse developments
- Confidentiality, privilege handling, joint defense/common interest protocol
- Conflicts: ongoing duty to disclose, prosecution bar acknowledgment, positional conflicts
- Document management, work product ownership, and **file transition/return obligations on termination**
- Vendor and e-discovery selection subject to company approval (in IP litigation this is frequently the single largest line item)
- Diversity and staffing-quality expectations, if applicable to your program

**Artifact 2 — Budget and Billing Controls**
- Phase-based budget by litigation stage (pleadings, discovery, claim construction/Markman, expert, dispositive motions, pretrial, trial, appeal) with variance thresholds triggering advance discussion
- Rate freeze term and rate-increase approval process
- Billing increments, block-billing prohibition, task-code requirements, detail sufficient to assess **contribution to the matter**
- Non-billable categories: file familiarization for new timekeepers, internal conferencing above a stated cap, administrative and clerical time, training
- Expense rules: travel class, meals, copying, research database charges, expert and vendor pass-throughs
- Invoice timing, audit rights, dispute and adjustment process
- Advance approval threshold for substantial expenses

**Artifact 3 — Evaluation Framework**
Scored against business-relevant criteria, not activity levels: results achieved; exposure reduced; cost control and budget accuracy; cycle time; communication quality and early warning; staffing quality and continuity; creativity and willingness to share risk. Includes a scorecard, review cadence, and a documented decision point for continuation, expansion, or transition.

---

**Assumptions:** You have authority to impose company-side guidelines on the firm; no third party currently controls the defense.

**Unknown/Insufficient data:** Matter posture, venue, firm identity, fee structure, budget envelope, internal approval thresholds, e-billing capability, existing OCG baseline, insurance and indemnity position.

**Key risks:** Preservation obligation may already have attached; insurance notice deadline; unmanaged e-discovery and expert spend; prosecution bar and conflicts exposure; guidelines superseded by the firm's engagement letter absent a precedence clause.

**Escalation triggers:** Pending or threatened litigation; potential insurance notice; conflicts or prosecution bar issues at the selected firm.

**Recommended next steps:** Confirm preservation and insurance status now; answer the clarifying set; then I draft the three artifacts.

**Needs current legal verification:** Yes — enforceability of specific guideline terms, prosecution bar scope, and any ethics-rule constraints on fee arrangements are **jurisdiction-specific review required**.

**Needs specialist or outside counsel review:** Yes — the final OCG and any alternative fee arrangement should be reviewed against applicable professional responsibility rules before execution.
