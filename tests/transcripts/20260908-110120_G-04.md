## 3. What the fragment already tells me — and it is escalation-grade

Even from one line:

- **"Alleges VP falsified reports."** If "reports" means financial, regulatory, safety or quality reports, this is potentially accounting/financial-reporting misconduct or regulatory false-statement exposure. Falsification allegations against a senior officer typically route **outside the accused's reporting line** — commonly to the General Counsel, Chief Compliance Officer and, depending on subject matter and your charters, the Audit Committee. Check your governing documents; I do not have them.
- **Reporter is identified.** Retaliation risk is live from this moment. No adverse action, schedule change, access change, performance step or "reassignment for their own benefit" involving this individual should occur without legal review.
- **Whistleblower protections may attach** without magic words and regardless of whether the allegation is ultimately substantiated. **Requires current legal verification** (e.g., SOX §806, Dodd-Frank, state analogues, or non-US equivalents — depends on jurisdiction and entity type, which are unknown).
- **Preservation action should be considered now.** An internal investigation into alleged falsification of reports is a classic anticipated-litigation/regulatory-inquiry trigger.

### Immediate preservation scoping (do this before further summarizing)

- **Custodians:** reporter; accused VP; VP's direct reports and assistant; anyone who prepared, reviewed, approved or received the reports at issue; relevant finance/quality/EHS/controller personnel.
- **Date span:** period covered by the allegedly falsified reports, plus a buffer before and after. Cannot be set without the notes.
- **Systems:** email; chat/messaging (including ephemeral or auto-delete channels — suspend auto-deletion); shared drives; the reporting/ERP/GL or quality system of record; version histories and audit logs; local devices; calendars; backups; HR file.
- **Record-keeping:** log who received the hold, when, how, acknowledgments and any updates.
- Suspend routine destruction for in-scope material. **Never delete, alter or "clean up" anything**, including the interview notes themselves.
- Consider whether the accused VP's system access or ability to alter the records at issue needs to be addressed — that is a decision for the investigation owner with IT and HR, and it must be handled without tipping off or creating a retaliation optic against the reporter.
