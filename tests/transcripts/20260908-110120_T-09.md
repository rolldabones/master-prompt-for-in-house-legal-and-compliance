## Clarifying questions

Answer what you can; mark anything else **Unknown** and I will proceed with stated assumptions.

**1. Objective and posture**
Are you trying to (a) enable adoption with guardrails, (b) restrict and control current shadow use, or (c) both — legitimize what is already happening while closing the worst gaps? Is there a known shadow-use problem today?

**2. Footprint and jurisdiction**
Where are employees located? Specifically: any EU/UK, California, Colorado, Illinois or NYC presence? Any regulated sector exposure (financial services, healthcare, insurance, defense/export-controlled, public sector)?

**3. Tool landscape**
What is already approved or in use — enterprise-licensed tools with contractual data protections, embedded AI features inside existing platforms (Microsoft, Google, Salesforce, GitHub), consumer/free accounts, or personal accounts on company work? Does the policy need to cover AI features that arrive automatically inside tools you already license?

**4. Data classification**
Do you have an existing data classification scheme I should map to? Which data classes are in play: personal data, customer confidential data under NDA, source code, trade secrets, financial pre-release information, HR/employee data, health data, export-controlled technical data?

**5. Use cases in scope**
Is this general productivity only, or does it need to cover: code generation, customer-facing outputs, marketing content, contract drafting/review, and — importantly — **any use touching hiring, promotion, performance, discipline or termination**? The employment-decision use case triggers a materially different legal regime and I would recommend handling it separately.

**6. Ownership and approval path**
Who owns the policy, and who decides whether a tool is approved? Is there an existing vendor risk / procurement intake I should plug into rather than build a parallel process? Do you want a standing AI review group or a single accountable owner?

**7. Intake mechanics**
What system will host the request workflow (ServiceNow, Jira, SharePoint form, email inbox)? Do you want a tiered process — light-touch self-service for low-risk use of already-approved tools, full review for new tools or sensitive data?

**8. Deliverable and timeline**
First draft for internal review, or near-final for approval? Do you want the full set (policy + standards/guidance + intake form + review workflow + tool register + attestation/training language), or a subset first? What is the deadline and who approves?

## What I would build once aligned

So you know what you are confirming:

1. **AI Use Policy** — purpose, scope (including contractors and embedded AI features), definitions, permitted/prohibited uses, data rules tied to your classification scheme, human review and accountability requirements, disclosure requirements, roles and responsibilities, exceptions, enforcement, review cadence.
2. **Tiered intake and approval process** — request form, triage criteria, review responsibilities by function, decision record, conditions of approval, re-review triggers.
3. **Approved tool register** — tool, owner, approved data classes, approved use cases, contractual protections confirmed, expiry/review date.
4. **Supporting artifacts** — employee quick-reference "do/don't" one-pager, attestation language, manager guidance.

## Early flags worth deciding on now

These recur in every version of this policy and are easy to miss:

- **Vendor terms on training and retention.** Whether inputs are used for model training, how long prompts and outputs are retained, and where processed. Consumer tiers and enterprise tiers differ sharply. This is a contract-review item, not a policy-drafting item.
- **Confidentiality obligations owed to third parties.** Customer NDAs and DPAs may prohibit disclosure to subprocessors or require notice. Policy compliance does not cure a contract breach.
- **IP in outputs.** Ownership and protectability of AI-generated material, and contamination risk in code (open-source and licensing). **Requires current legal verification** and **Jurisdiction-specific review required.**
- **Privilege.** Legal and compliance use of AI on privileged matters needs its own rule and its own approved environment.
- **Records and preservation.** Prompts, chat logs and outputs are business records and are potentially discoverable. Retention periods and litigation-hold scoping should be addressed in the policy, not discovered during a dispute. **Preservation action should be considered now** if any matter is already under hold — confirm whether existing holds capture AI tool logs.
- **Employment decisions.** Use of AI in hiring, screening, promotion or discipline engages bias-audit, notice and automated-decision rules in several jurisdictions and, in the EU, high-risk classification. I recommend a default prohibition pending separate specialist review.
- **Human accountability.** The policy should state plainly that the employee using the tool owns the output and its accuracy. This is the single most useful sentence in most AI policies.
