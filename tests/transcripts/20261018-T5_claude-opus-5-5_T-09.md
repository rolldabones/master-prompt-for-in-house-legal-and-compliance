## Why this matters

An AI-use policy touches several sensitive areas at once:

- **Confidentiality and trade secrets:** information entered into a tool may leave company control.
- **Privacy:** personal data may go to third-party models, and cross-border transfers may occur.
- **IP:** who owns the outputs, whether they infringe others' rights, and whether the vendor trains on your inputs.
- **Privilege:** legal content entered into unapproved tools could be exposed.
- **Employment:** AI used in hiring, performance or discipline decisions is regulated in some jurisdictions.
- **Securities:** material nonpublic information could be entered into external tools.
- **Records and preservation:** prompts and outputs may be discoverable records subject to litigation holds.

AI law is also changing quickly. Any jurisdiction-specific content will be marked **Requires current legal verification**.

## Clarifying questions

1. **Jurisdictions:** Where do you have employees and operations? This determines whether the EU AI Act, GDPR, works-council consultation, or US state and local rules on automated employment decisions apply.
   *Default: US-headquartered, with a flag for non-US review.*

2. **Industry and regulation:** Are you in a regulated sector such as financial services, healthcare, government contracting or critical infrastructure? Are you a public company?
   *Default: not heavily regulated. Treat MNPI as a risk regardless.*

3. **Current state:**
   - Are any AI tools already approved or licensed (for example, an enterprise Copilot or ChatGPT Enterprise)?
   - Do you know of unsanctioned use?
   - Has anything gone wrong yet?

   *Default: some enterprise tools exist, unsanctioned use is likely, and there has been no known incident.*

4. **Scope:**
   - Generative AI only, or all AI, including AI features embedded in existing vendor software?
   - Employees only, or also contractors and temps?
   - Internal productivity use only, or also AI in customer-facing products?

   *Default: all AI tools used for work, covering employees and contractors, internal use only. Product development handled separately.*

5. **Risk posture:** Permissive ("approved tools allowed, with guardrails") or restrictive ("prohibited unless approved for a specific use case")?
   *Default: tiered. Approved tools can be used for low-risk work. High-risk uses need intake review. Some uses are prohibited.*

6. **Intake model:** Should intake approve **tools**, **use cases**, or both?
   *Default: both. Tool approval goes through security, privacy and procurement. Use-case review is required for high-risk uses such as employment decisions, personal data, customer-facing output or regulated data.*

7. **Ownership and approvers:**
   - Who will own the policy?
   - Who reviews intake requests (for example, IT/Security, Privacy, Legal, Procurement, HR)?
   - Is there an existing AI governance committee?

   *Default: Legal/Compliance owns the policy, and a cross-functional review group approves requests.*

8. **Existing policies to align with:** Can you share your data classification, information security, acceptable use, privacy, vendor risk and records retention policies? The AI policy should reference them rather than duplicate them.
   *Default: I'll draft placeholders where those policies would be cross-referenced.*

9. **Systems:** Will intake run through a ticketing tool (ServiceNow, Jira), a form, or email?
   *Default: tool-agnostic form fields and workflow.*

10. **Deliverable, audience and timing:**
    - Do you want a plain-English employee policy plus a separate internal procedure, or one combined document?
    - Do you have a target date?
    - Do you need works council or union consultation?

    *Default: two documents. An employee-facing policy of about 3–4 pages, and an intake procedure with a request form, a risk-tiering rubric and a RACI chart.*
