## Direct answer

I can't tell you it's OK to sign, and no one should on this record. Two reasons:

1. **I don't know which side you're on**, what the rest of the agreement says, or the governing law. All three change the answer completely.
2. **As drafted, this clause is not market-standard in either direction.** It is unusually broad, procedurally incomplete, and creates cap-avoidance and insurance-coverage problems even for the party it appears to favor.

Directional view: **if you are the Supplier, do not sign this as-is.** If you are the Customer, it is favorable on scope but defective on mechanics and may be partly unenforceable or uninsurable — which means it may not deliver the protection it appears to give.

## Red flags in the clause as drafted

**1. No "third-party claim" limitation.** Standard indemnities cover third-party claims. This one doesn't say so. As written it can be read to cover *first-party* claims — i.e., Customer's own breach-of-contract claims against Supplier get recharacterized as indemnity claims. That matters because indemnity claims often sit outside the liability cap and outside the consequential-damages waiver, and they bypass ordinary contract damages doctrines. This is the single biggest drafting defect.

**2. "Any and all claims arising out of or relating to this Agreement" is untethered from fault.** It is not limited to Supplier's breach, negligence, IP infringement, or misconduct. On its face it captures claims caused entirely by **Customer's** own negligence, breach, specifications or misuse. Whether that is enforceable is **jurisdiction-specific review required** — many jurisdictions require express, conspicuous language to indemnify a party for its own negligence, and some (construction, oilfield, certain services) have anti-indemnity statutes that void it outright.

**3. "Relating to" is broader than "arising out of."** Courts generally read "relating to" expansively. Combined with "any and all," the outer boundary of this obligation is effectively undefined.

**4. Duty to defend has no procedure.** The defense obligation is typically triggered by *allegation*, not proven liability, and is broader and more expensive than the indemnity itself. This clause contains none of the standard machinery:
- notice requirements and consequences of late notice
- who controls the defense and selects counsel
- consent rights over settlement (both directions)
- cooperation obligations
- what happens on mixed/partly-covered claims
- right to assume defense at own cost if the indemnitor won't

**5. No definition of recoverable amounts.** "Claims" alone is ambiguous. Standard drafting covers losses, damages, liabilities, judgments, settlements, penalties, and **reasonable** attorneys' fees and costs. As drafted, the recoverable scope is arguable in both directions.

**6. Unknown interaction with the rest of the agreement.** I have not seen the limitation of liability, insurance, IP, warranty or exclusive-remedy provisions. Critical unknowns: is indemnity carved out of the liability cap? Is it capped separately? Is it excluded from the consequential-damages waiver? Is it stated to be the sole remedy? **Reviewing an indemnity in isolation is not a reliable review.**

**7. Insurance mismatch.** Broad, fault-independent contractual indemnity frequently exceeds what standard CGL "insured contract" contractual-liability coverage will pick up. If you're the Supplier, you may be assuming uninsured balance-sheet risk. If you're the Customer, you may be relying on an indemnity your counterparty cannot fund. Broker/risk-management confirmation needed either way.

**8. No mutuality and no carve-outs.** No reciprocal Customer indemnity — notably for Customer-supplied specifications, materials, data or content, which is standard even in customer-favorable contracts.

**9. No survival clause reference.** Indemnities normally need express survival past termination.

## What I need before I can give you a usable answer

1. **Which party are you?** Supplier or Customer.
2. **Governing law and venue** stated in the agreement.
3. **The full agreement**, or at minimum: limitation of liability, insurance, warranty, IP, confidentiality, termination and survival clauses.
4. **What is being supplied** — goods, software, professional services, staffing, on-site work? (On-site/construction/staffing sharply raises anti-indemnity and insurance issues.)
5. **Contract value and exposure profile** — annual spend, criticality, whether Supplier's people work on your premises, whether personal data or IP is involved.
6. **Negotiating posture** — is this your paper or theirs? How much leverage do you have? Is there an approved fallback position or a company contracting standard/playbook?
7. **Deadline and decision owner** — who signs, and under what approval authority?
8. **Do you want current-law verification** on enforceability in the governing jurisdiction?

## Illustrative redraft (balanced starting point — not advice, and not tuned until I know your side)

> Supplier shall defend, indemnify and hold harmless Customer, its affiliates, and their respective officers, directors and employees from and against any **third-party** claim, demand, suit or proceeding ("**Claim**"), and all resulting losses, damages, liabilities, settlements, judgments, penalties, and reasonable attorneys' fees and costs, **to the extent arising out of** (a) Supplier's breach of this Agreement, (b) Supplier's negligence or willful misconduct, or (c) any allegation that the Deliverables infringe or misappropriate any third-party intellectual property right.
>
> Supplier shall have **no obligation** under this Section to the extent a Claim arises from (i) Customer's negligence or willful misconduct, (ii) Customer's breach of this Agreement, (iii) Customer-supplied specifications, materials or data, or (iv) modification or use of the Deliverables other than as authorized.
>
> **Procedure.** Customer shall promptly notify Supplier of any Claim (failure to do so relieving Supplier only to the extent prejudiced), grant Supplier sole control of the defense and settlement with counsel of Supplier's choosing, and provide reasonable cooperation at Supplier's expense. Supplier shall not settle any Claim in a manner that imposes non-monetary obligations on, or admits liability of, Customer without Customer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld. Customer may participate at its own expense with counsel of its choosing.

Key deliberate changes: third-party limitation, fault linkage, **"to the extent"** (enables proportionate allocation on mixed claims), express carve-outs, and full defense procedure.

**Supplier-side additional asks:** cap the indemnity (or at least the non-IP indemnity) at a defined multiple of fees; exclude consequential/indirect damages from indemnified losses; add standard IP-infringement remedies (repair/replace/refund + termination) as sole remedy for IP claims.

**Customer-side additional asks:** carve the indemnity out of the liability cap (or set a higher super-cap); require insurance backing with additional-insured and waiver-of-subrogation endorsements and primary/non-contributory wording; add a data-breach/privacy indemnity if personal data is in scope.
