# T-06 Compliance Program

**Model:** claude-opus-5  **Run:** 20260908-110120

## User message

A regulator asked for evidence that our anti-bribery compliance program actually works. We have a policy on the intranet. What do we show them?

## Assistant response

**Mode: TRIAGE → COMPLIANCE PROGRAM → LITIGATION RESPONSE (regulator-facing)**

A regulator asking for effectiveness evidence is an escalation event, not a documentation exercise. Before scoping the answer, four things need to happen immediately.

---

## 1. Immediate flags

**Preservation action should be considered now.** A regulator inquiry into anti-bribery compliance is a preservation trigger. Suspend routine destruction across compliance, finance, internal audit, HR and the relevant business units before any collection begins.

**Do not create, back-date, revise or "tidy up" any document to close a gap.** This is the single largest risk in a request of this type. Altering, back-dating or generating retrospective records for a regulator can convert a compliance weakness into an obstruction or false-statement exposure that is far worse than the underlying gap. Anything produced must be what actually existed, dated as it actually was. If a document is created now to describe the program, label it as prepared in response to the request.

**Do not respond, produce or communicate substantively with the regulator until scope and channel are set by counsel.** Confirm who owns the relationship, what exactly was asked, in what form, and under what authority.

**Escalate to specialist or outside counsel.** Anti-bribery regulator engagement is specialist work and is jurisdiction-specific. If outside counsel is not engaged, that decision should be made now, not after a first response is sent.

**Privilege:** risk assessments, internal audit findings, investigation files and prior gap analyses may be privileged or may contain damaging admissions. Do not hand these over reflexively. Privilege positioning, waiver risk and the distinction between factual production and legal analysis need to be handled deliberately.

---

## 2. Questions that change the answer

I need these before recommending a specific production strategy:

1. **Which regulator, which jurisdiction, and under what authority** was the request made?
2. **What form did the request take** — informal supervisory question, exam request list, formal demand, subpoena, self-disclosure follow-up, monitorship, tender/licensing/debarment screening?
3. **Is there a known underlying trigger** — an allegation, a whistleblower, a media report, a counterparty issue, a related enforcement matter, a third-party's case? Regulators rarely ask this question in the abstract.
4. **What is the stated deadline**, and is extension possible?
5. **What actually exists beyond the policy** — training records, third-party due diligence files, risk assessments, hotline data, audit or monitoring work, investigation records?
6. **Who inside the company already knows,** and has anything been said or sent to the regulator already?
7. **Is outside counsel engaged, and is insurance notice potentially relevant** (D&O, in some structures)?

---

## 3. The framing problem

"We have a policy on the intranet" answers the wrong question. Regulators evaluating anti-bribery programs generally test three separate things, and evidence for one does not substitute for another:

| Layer | Question | A policy on the intranet |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** | Is the program well designed for *this* company's actual risk profile? | Partial evidence at best |
| **Implementation** | Is it applied in good faith, resourced and empowered? | No evidence |
| **Operating effectiveness** | Does it work in practice — is it tested, does it detect, does it lead to consequences? | No evidence |

Most major anti-bribery enforcement frameworks are structured around some version of these three layers. The specific framework, its current version and its expectations are **jurisdiction-specific and require current legal verification** — do not build a response against a remembered version of any guidance document.

---

## 4. Provisional evidence inventory

Use this as a gap-triage worksheet first, and a production candidate list second. For each row, ask: *does this exist, is it dated, is it retrievable, and does it show operation rather than intention?*

| Program element | Evidence that shows it **operates** |
|---|---|
| **Risk assessment** | Dated bribery-specific risk assessments; methodology; country/third-party/transaction risk scoring; evidence that findings changed controls, resourcing or coverage |
| **Tone from the top** | Board and audit committee minutes referencing bribery risk; CEO/senior leader communications with dates and distribution; evidence of decisions where compliance concerns overrode commercial pressure |
| **Autonomy and resources** | Compliance reporting line and org chart; headcount and budget over time; direct access to the board or audit committee; qualifications of compliance personnel; evidence compliance can escalate without business veto |
| **Policies and procedures** | Version history and approval records; acknowledgment rates by population; localized and translated versions; gifts/hospitality, facilitation payments, charitable and political contribution procedures |
| **Training and communication** | Completion data by role and risk tier; risk-based targeting (not just annual all-staff e-learning); content; testing scores; live sessions for high-risk populations; non-completion follow-up |
| **Third-party management** | Due diligence files for agents, intermediaries, distributors, consultants; risk-tiered diligence; documented business rationale for intermediaries; contractual anti-bribery clauses and audit rights; ongoing monitoring and re-screening; evidence of relationships declined or terminated |
| **Financial and books-and-records controls** | Payment approval matrices; segregation of duties; controls over cash, petty cash, expenses, discounts, rebates, commissions; testing of those controls |
| **Reporting channels** | Hotline availability, languages, anonymity option; volume and trend data; awareness data; retaliation protections and how they are enforced |
| **Investigations** | Case log with intake, triage, timeliness, outcomes; investigation protocol; independence of investigators; root-cause analysis |
| **Discipline and incentives** | Anonymized records of discipline actually imposed for bribery or control breaches, including senior personnel; compensation and promotion criteria that reflect compliance conduct |
| **Monitoring, testing and audit** | Internal audit plan including bribery-specific coverage; audit reports and findings; control testing results; data analytics or transaction monitoring; closure of findings |
| **M&A and integration** | Pre-acquisition anti-bribery diligence; post-close integration and remediation records |
| **Continuous improvement** | Evidence of the loop closing: an issue arose → root cause identified → control changed → change tested |

The strongest single category is the last one. Evidence that the program **found something, and the company then changed something**, does more than any volume of policy documents.

---

## 5. If there are real gaps

Likely, given the starting point. The general posture that holds up best:

- **Do not overstate.** A misstatement to a regulator is its own offense and is often more damaging than the gap it conceals.
- **Be accurate about what exists, when it was created, and what state it is in.**
- **Present a credible, dated, resourced remediation plan** with named owners and milestones — but only one the company will actually execute. A remediation plan that is presented and then not delivered is worse than none.
- **Distinguish "we did not do this" from "we did this but did not document it."** The second is recoverable if there is corroborating evidence (calendar entries, emails, system logs, attendance records). Reconstruct honestly and label reconstructions as such.

Whether, how and when to present gaps and remediation to this particular regulator is a strategic decision that belongs with outside counsel, not with a generic framework.

4. Run the inventory above internally as a gap assessment — under privilege where available.
