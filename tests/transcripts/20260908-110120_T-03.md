## Do these now, regardless of the notice

These are protective and reversible. They do not require me to finish drafting.

1. **Suspend auto-deletion** on email, messaging (Teams/Slack/SMS/WhatsApp if used operationally), and any short-retention systems for the likely custodian group. Tell IT in writing; keep the request.
2. **Preserve physical evidence:** the equipment, machine, guard, tooling, PPE, materials, and the scene condition. Do not repair, discard, return-to-vendor, or reconfigure without documenting first.
   - *If a safety hazard requires immediate correction, correct it — but photograph, video, measure and log the pre-change condition first, and record who authorized the change and why.*
3. **Preserve video** — plant CCTV and body/dash cameras typically overwrite on short cycles. This is the single most commonly lost category in plant matters. Pull and copy now.
4. **Preserve machine/system data:** PLC/SCADA logs, maintenance management system (CMMS) records, badge access, time and attendance, alarm histories, sensor/telemetry. Many of these roll over.
5. **Consider insurance notice** (GL, EPL, property, umbrella, D&O if management conduct is implicated). Late notice is a recurring, avoidable loss.
6. **Communication discipline:** instruct that root-cause discussion, blame assignment and speculation stay off email/chat and go through counsel.

**Preservation action should be considered now.**

## Questions I need answered to draft (short version)

**Trigger**
1. What happened, in one or two sentences — injury, fatality, near-miss, chemical release, equipment failure, fire, repeat citation?
2. What triggered the hold: incident occurrence, employee complaint, demand letter, agency inspection or citation, subpoena, union grievance, media inquiry, internal report?
3. Date of incident and date the company first learned of it.

**Scope**
4. Which facility/facilities and which lines, cells, departments or shifts?
5. Relevant date range — I default to a start date well before the incident to capture maintenance, training and prior-complaint history.
6. Was a contractor, staffing agency, vendor or equipment manufacturer involved? (Third-party preservation letters may be needed — different document.)

**Custodians and systems**
7. Approximate custodian list or the roles: operator(s), supervisor, shift lead, maintenance techs, EHS manager, plant manager, HR, engineering, procurement for the equipment.
8. Do plant personnel use personal devices or personal messaging for work? Are there paper-only records (shift logs, JSAs, LOTO tags, permits, pre-shift checklists)?

**Governance**
9. Jurisdiction (state/country) and whether the plant is unionized.
10. Who signs the notice — GC, EHS-side counsel, or outside counsel? Is outside counsel engaged?
11. Is this being handled as privileged/at counsel's direction? Has an investigation started, and is it legal-directed?
12. Has any agency (OSHA-equivalent, environmental, state) already contacted the company or opened an inspection?
