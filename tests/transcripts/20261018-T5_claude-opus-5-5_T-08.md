## Clarifying questions

**1. The matter**
- Are we plaintiff or defendant?
- What type of IP: patent, trade secret, copyright, trademark, or mixed?
- What forum: U.S. district court (which district), ITC, PTAB/IPR, non-U.S. court, or parallel proceedings?
- What stage: pre-filing, just served, or underway?

**2. The fee arrangement**
- Is it hourly, phased budget, capped or collared fees, fixed fee per phase, or a hybrid or contingent structure?
- How sensitive is the budget? Is there a ceiling management has already set?
- Is there third-party litigation funding?

**3. Insurance**
- Could any policy respond, such as IP infringement coverage or a CGL "advertising injury" provision?
- If so, has notice been given? Insurer counsel guidelines may override or constrain yours.

**4. What already exists**
- Does the company have a standard outside counsel guidelines template?
- Do you use an e-billing platform (for example, LEDES or UTBMS codes)?
- Is there a draft engagement letter from the firm?

**5. Internal ownership**
- Who will direct the firm day to day?
- Who approves budget changes, settlement discussions, expert retention and staffing changes?
- Which business, finance and executive stakeholders need reporting?

**6. Other players**
- Will there be co-counsel, local counsel, separate PTAB counsel, experts, consultants or an e-discovery vendor?
- Is this a transition from a prior firm?

**7. Deliverable and timing**
- Do you want one matter-specific package, or a general guidelines document plus a matter addendum?
- Who reads it: just the firm, or internal stakeholders too?
- When does retention need to be finalized?

## Proposed deliverable package (to confirm)

1. **Outside counsel guidelines.** This would cover:
   - Scope and authority, including actions that need prior approval.
   - Staffing rules and rate controls.
   - Communication and reporting cadence.
   - Billing format and non-billable items.
   - Expense rules and thresholds for advance approval.
   - Conflicts, including positional conflicts, which are common in patent work.
   - Confidentiality, data security and privilege handling.
2. **Budget controls.**
   - A phased budget template keyed to IP litigation stages: pleadings and early motions, claim construction or *Markman*, fact discovery, experts, dispositive motions, trial, and appeal or post-grant proceedings.
   - Variance thresholds that trigger notice and re-approval.
   - Accrual and forecast reporting.
3. **Evaluation scorecard.**
   - Criteria: results, cost control, cycle time, reduction in exposure, communication quality, staffing quality, and creativity and risk sharing.
   - Defined metrics, a review cadence and an end-of-matter review.
4. **Engagement letter checklist.** The terms that should appear in the retention letter itself, so the guidelines are actually binding.

## Flags to consider now, regardless of drafting

- **Preservation action should be considered now** if litigation is filed or reasonably anticipated. Confirm whether a hold has issued and who owns it.
- **Insurance notice.** If any policy could respond, late notice can jeopardize coverage. Consider notice promptly.
- **Conflicts.** Ask the firm for a conflicts clearance that specifically addresses positional and prosecution conflicts, such as prosecuting patents for competitors in the same technology.
- **Protective order and prosecution bar.** In patent cases, in-house lawyers involved in prosecution may be restricted from seeing opposing confidential information. This affects who can supervise counsel.
- **Data handling.** Before pasting the engagement letter, budgets or case facts here, confirm this tool is approved for privileged and confidential data.
