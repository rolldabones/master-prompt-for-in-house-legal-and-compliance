## 1. Do these now (today or tomorrow)

**a. Lock down the deadline. Do not rely on "21 days" yet.**
- Confirm the **exact date and method of service** and **who received it** (registered agent, an officer, the front desk). The date and method can change the deadline.
- Confirm the **court**:
  - **Federal or state.** 21 days matches the general federal rule for a served defendant. State rules vary, and waiver of service changes federal timing.
  - **If state court:** removal to federal court may be an option, and it typically has its own short deadline that runs from service. Outside counsel needs to assess this immediately.
- Calendar the deadline conservatively, counting from the service date, with internal reminders at day 7 and day 14.
- The response may be an answer **or a motion** (to dismiss, to compel arbitration, to transfer). Counsel decides which, and that takes lead time.
- **Requires current legal verification. Jurisdiction-specific review required.**

**b. Engage outside counsel this week.**
- A corporation generally cannot represent itself in court. Counsel must be admitted in the forum.
- Run conflict checks against the plaintiff and any related parties.
- Ask counsel up front whether a **stipulated extension** with opposing counsel is realistic. It is common, but never assume it until it is agreed and, where required, filed or approved.

**c. Preservation action should be considered now.**
- The preservation duty has attached at least as of service, and possibly earlier if a dispute was reasonably anticipated before the complaint.
- Issue a **litigation hold** to likely custodians:
  - deal team and negotiators
  - relationship and account managers
  - operations staff who performed under the contract
  - finance and billing
  - any executives involved
  - legal and contracts staff
- **Suspend auto-deletion** for email, Teams/Slack, texts on company and personal devices used for work, shared drives, CRM, ERP and billing systems, and backups if relevant.
- Keep a hold log covering recipients, date sent, method, acknowledgments and updates.

**d. Check insurance and tender to third parties.**
- Pull the policies: E&O / professional liability, D&O (entity coverage), cyber and CGL. Pure contract claims are often excluded, but do not decide that yourself. Policies frequently require notice "as soon as practicable," and **late notice can forfeit coverage.**
- Notify your broker promptly.
- Check whether any vendor, subcontractor or partner owes you **contractual indemnity** for this claim. If so, tender to them in writing.

**e. Pull the contract and read the dispute provisions before anyone responds.** Look for:
- **Arbitration clause.** Litigating on the merits can risk waiving the right to arbitrate. This is one of the most important early checks.
- Forum selection and choice of law
- Notice and cure requirements, which the plaintiff may have skipped
- Limitation of liability, damages caps and consequential damages waivers
- Jury waiver, fee-shifting and contractual limitations periods

**f. Impose communication discipline.**
- No one discusses the lawsuit with the plaintiff, the plaintiff's counsel or third parties except through counsel.
- If the business relationship is ongoing, agree on a script with counsel for ordinary-course contact.
- Instruct the internal team not to create new commentary about the dispute in email or chat. Route questions to Legal. Mark legal communications as privileged where appropriate, recognizing that the label alone does not create privilege.

## 2. Questions I need answered

1. Which **court and jurisdiction**? State or federal?
2. **Date, method and recipient** of service?
3. Who is the **plaintiff**, what is the **relationship** (customer, vendor, partner), and is it ongoing?
4. **Amount claimed**, and what other relief is sought?
5. Do we have the **contract**? Does it contain an arbitration, forum or notice-and-cure clause?
6. Did we have **claims against them**? Some counterclaims may be compulsory, meaning they must be raised in the response or are lost.
7. Is the company **public**, or subject to lender, auditor or board reporting thresholds? Material litigation may carry disclosure or reporting implications.
8. Do you have **existing outside counsel** for this kind of matter, or do you need selection help?
9. Who internally is the **decision owner** and **approver** for retaining counsel and authorizing spend?
