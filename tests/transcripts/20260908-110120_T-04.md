# T-04 Investigation Support

**Model:** claude-opus-5  **Run:** 20260908-110120

## User message

Our hotline received a report that a VP of Sales has been approving fake expense reports for a vendor owned by his cousin. Help me get started.

## Assistant response

## Mode

**Mode: TRIAGE → INVESTIGATION SUPPORT → likely ESCALATION**

This is not a routine matter. On the facts as described, it involves alleged fraud by a senior officer, a related-party/conflict-of-interest dimension, and potential control failures in the expense approval process. Treat it as material and privilege-sensitive from the outset.

---

## Before we go further — two cautions

**1. Data handling.** Do not paste the hotline report verbatim, the complainant's identity, or personnel/financial records into this tool unless your organization has confirmed it is approved for privileged, personal and investigation-sensitive data. Work from de-identified summaries if you are unsure.

**2. Privilege posture.** Decide *now*, before any substantive work begins, whether this investigation is being conducted at the direction of counsel for the purpose of providing legal advice. That decision is much harder to reconstruct later. Label materials accordingly and keep circulation need-to-know.

---

## No-regrets actions you can take today

These are safe to start before the full picture is clear:

1. **Preservation.** *Preservation action should be considered now.* An internal fraud allegation against a named individual, involving identifiable transactions, is a reasonably anticipated dispute trigger. See scoping below.
2. **Lock the intake record.** Preserve the hotline report in the complainant's original words. Do not paraphrase over it. Record date/time received, channel, and whether the reporter is identified or anonymous.
3. **Close the circle.** Identify everyone who already knows and stop further distribution. Every additional person increases tip-off risk, retaliation risk and privilege risk.
4. **Do not contact the VP or the vendor.** No interviews, no "informal chats," no questions to the VP's team, no vendor inquiries until scoping and preservation are done.
5. **Retaliation watch.** If the reporter is identified or reasonably identifiable, flag retaliation risk immediately and note any pending performance actions, transfers or terminations affecting them or their likely colleagues.
6. **Escalation clock.** Determine today who must be told and when — at minimum the General Counsel or CCO. If the company is public or has an audit committee with fraud-reporting obligations, senior-management fraud is typically a mandatory escalation. **Requires current legal verification** and review of your charters and policies.

---

## Preliminary preservation scope

**Custodians (starting set):**
- The VP of Sales
- The VP's direct reports and administrative support
- Anyone in the approval chain above and below the VP for these expenses
- AP / expense-processing staff who touched the transactions
- Vendor-onboarding / procurement personnel who approved the vendor
- The complainant (preserve, do not collect intrusively)

**Systems and repositories:**
- Email, calendar, corporate chat/messaging (including any ephemeral or auto-delete channels — suspend auto-deletion)
- Expense management system (submissions, approvals, receipts, audit trail/metadata)
- AP and ERP records: vendor master file, vendor onboarding documents, W-9/tax records, banking details and change history, invoices, payment records
- Procurement records: contracts, POs, statements of work, competitive-bid or sole-source justifications
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure records for the VP
- HR file for the VP, including any prior complaints
- Personal-device and BYOD considerations — **Jurisdiction-specific review required**
- Backups and any offsite/archived storage

**Date span:** Provisionally from the vendor's onboarding date to present. Widen if the VP's tenure predates onboarding.

**Critical:** suspend routine destruction/auto-purge across these systems *before* anyone is put on notice. Document who received the hold, when, how, and acknowledgments.

**Tip-off risk:** A broadly circulated hold notice may alert the subject. Consider a narrow, IT-side preservation (system-level, no user notice) first, followed by a scoped hold notice once collection is secured. Sequencing here should be decided with counsel.

## What I need from you to build the investigation plan

**On the allegation:**
1. What exactly does the report say — what conduct, over what period, involving what dollar magnitude? (Summarized is fine.)
2. Is the reporter identified, anonymous, or anonymous-but-identifiable? Are they inside the VP's organization?
3. Does the report allege the *vendor is fake* (no services rendered), *services rendered but inflated*, *undisclosed related party but legitimate services*, or some combination? These are very different matters.
4. What, if anything, has already been done in response? Has anyone spoken to anyone?

**On the company context:**
5. Is the company public, private, PE/VC-backed, or otherwise subject to external reporting? Any pending audit, financing, M&A process or covenant certification?
6. Which jurisdiction(s) — company HQ, the VP's work location, the vendor's location?
7. Do you have a conflict-of-interest policy and a signed COI disclosure from this VP? A vendor code of conduct? A related-party transaction policy?
8. What is the VP's approval authority and delegation-of-authority limit? Who approves above him?
9. Is the vendor still being paid? Are payments imminent?
10. Is outside counsel or a forensic accountant already engaged? Is internal audit involved?
11. What does your incident-response / investigation policy require, and does the audit committee charter mandate reporting of alleged senior-management fraud?

**On decision rights:**
12. Who owns this matter, and who has authority to approve interviews, collections, suspension of the VP, and vendor payment holds?

## Issues I am already flagging

| Issue | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Fraud / misappropriation by an officer** | Potential criminal exposure; may trigger law-enforcement referral decisions later. |
| **Undisclosed related-party transaction** | Independent of whether services were real — COI policy breach and disclosure implications. |
| **Internal control failure** | If a VP could self-approve or circumvent controls, the control gap is a separate finding from the conduct. For public companies this can implicate ICFR. |
| **Financial statement impact** | Depends on magnitude and periods affected. May implicate auditors and prior-period reporting. |
| **Retaliation** | Highest near-term legal risk after the fraud itself. Whistleblower protections vary by jurisdiction and may be statutory. |
| **Privilege** | Must be structured now, not retrofitted. |
| **Insurance** | Fidelity/crime coverage and D&O may require prompt notice. Check policy notice conditions early. |
| **Employment action** | Any suspension or termination of the VP raises separate process, contract and jurisdictional questions. |
| **Vendor relationship** | Contract termination rights, clawback, recovery, and whether the vendor entity has other business with you. |

### Ending Block

**Assumptions:** Report is credible enough to warrant intake and preliminary assessment; no investigation steps have yet been taken; the VP is currently employed and in role; you have a functioning hotline and expense system with audit trails.

**Documents/data to gather next:** Hotline intake record; COI policy and the VP's disclosures; delegation of authority matrix; expense policy; vendor onboarding file and vendor master record; all payments to the vendor; expense reports and approval audit trail; VP's employment agreement; investigation policy; audit committee charter; crime and D&O policies.

**Needs specialist or outside counsel review:** Yes. Alleged fraud by a corporate officer with related-party and possible financial-reporting dimensions typically warrants outside counsel and forensic accounting support, both for independence and for privilege durability if the matter later becomes regulator- or auditor-facing.
