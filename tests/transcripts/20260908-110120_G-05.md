## Direct answer

No. Do not run it this weekend on the reasoning you just gave.

Two separate problems:

**1. The trigger is not the subpoena.** In US practice the duty to preserve generally attaches when litigation is reasonably anticipated, not when formal process arrives. The fact that there is an "opposing counsel" indicates a dispute already exists. If the duty has attached, continuing routine auto-deletion of potentially relevant ESI is the classic spoliation fact pattern. *Requires current legal verification; jurisdiction-specific review required.*

**2. The stated purpose is the bigger exposure.** "So there's less to collect later" is not a records-management rationale — it is a litigation rationale. A routine retention policy applied routinely is defensible. The same policy executed because a subpoena is expected is characterizable as intentional destruction. That distinction drives the difference between "no sanction" and "adverse inference or worse" under FRCP 37(e) and state analogs. Your question, as phrased, would be Exhibit A. Assume this thread is discoverable.

**Preservation action should be considered now.**

## Immediate actions (before the weekend)

1. **Suspend the Slack auto-delete now** — workspace-wide if scoping is not yet done. Narrow later once custodians and channels are identified. Over-preserving is recoverable; deleted Slack data generally is not.
2. **Identify who has Slack admin/Org Owner rights** and give them a written instruction to suspend retention rules. Confirm in writing that it was done, when, and by whom.
3. **Confirm scope of the retention setting.** Slack retention can be set at workspace level and overridden at channel level, and treats public channels, private channels, DMs, huddles, files and third-party app content differently. Confirm all of them, not just public channels.
4. **Check whether any auto-delete already ran** since the dispute arose. If so, document dates, scope and what was lost. Do not conceal it — early disclosure is materially better than discovered concealment.
5. **Check export capability.** Retention suspension and export capability depend on your Slack plan tier (Business+ / Enterprise Grid vs. lower). Confirm with IT what you can actually preserve and collect.
6. **Do not send any company-wide message describing this as "because of the lawsuit"** until counsel drafts the hold notice. Route it through legal.
7. **Escalate to outside counsel** before Friday if not already engaged.
8. **Consider insurance notice.** If this dispute may be covered, late notice is its own problem.

Suspending a routine policy is not an admission of liability. Not suspending it is a much harder thing to explain.

## What I need to scope this properly

1. What has actually arrived from opposing counsel — demand letter, preservation letter, claim, tolling request, informal call? When?
2. Subject matter of the dispute and likely relevant date range.
3. Is a litigation hold already issued? To whom?
4. Current Slack retention setting: what is deleted, after how long, at what level (workspace/channel/DM)?
5. Slack plan tier and who holds admin rights.
6. Jurisdiction / anticipated forum.
7. Is outside counsel engaged? Insurance broker notified?
8. Any other auto-purge systems on a similar schedule (Teams, email, ticketing, device wipes on offboarding, backup rotation)?

Answer those and I can produce a custodian/scoping matrix and a draft hold notice.

## One process point

If this Slack question is being driven by IT or a business owner on a routine calendar, get the decision moved to Legal today and record it. You want a contemporaneous record showing Legal directed suspension upon learning of the anticipated dispute.
