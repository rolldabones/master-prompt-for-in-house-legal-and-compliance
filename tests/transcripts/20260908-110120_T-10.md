## Decision framework: when this stops being an internal matter

Give the committee criteria, not just a conclusion. Factors that push materially toward **Option C — independent counsel reporting to the audit committee**:

- Public company and the conduct touches financial reporting, ICFR, or the expense/AP control environment
- The subject is senior enough that management's independence is questionable, or the subject reports to or influences anyone supervising the investigation
- Any indication of falsified records, vendor collusion, or concealment (as opposed to sloppy policy compliance)
- The external auditor is likely to ask "who investigated, and were they independent?"
- Dollar exposure is material, or scope is genuinely unknown
- Possible regulator, law enforcement referral, or public disclosure downstream
- The committee may later need to defend the adequacy and independence of the investigation

Factors that support **Option A/B**: bounded, well-documented, low-dollar, single-actor policy violation with no falsification, no reporting-line conflict, and no financial-statement or disclosure implication.

**Blunt point for the committee:** the cost of independent counsel is usually small relative to the cost of a later finding that the investigation was run by people the subject could influence. That framing belongs in the paper.

## Do these now, regardless of the counsel decision

1. **Preservation.** If no hold is out, issue one today — VP and assistants, direct reports, expense approvers, AP/T&E processing, card administrator. Systems: email, Teams/Slack, expense platform (Concur or equivalent), corporate card issuer records, ERP/AP, shared drives, phone/laptop, calendars. Suspend auto-delete. Log recipients, dates, acknowledgments. **Preservation action should be considered now.**
2. **Access.** Consider suspending card and expense-approval authority in a way that doesn't tip off or prejudge. Coordinate with HR — a step that looks like discipline before findings creates its own exposure.
3. **Insurance.** Fidelity/crime and D&O policies often have short notice windows. Check and consider notice promptly.
4. **External auditor.** Fraud involving a member of management is generally communicable to the auditor and may bear on ICFR conclusions. **Requires current legal verification** and coordination with the CFO/controller — but do not let the audit committee learn of it from the auditor first.
5. **Privilege hygiene.** Route the work through counsel now if it isn't already. Limit circulation of the briefing to committee members, GC, and CCO. No forwarding, no summarizing in general management decks.
6. **Complainant protection.** If there is a reporter, document anti-retaliation steps.
