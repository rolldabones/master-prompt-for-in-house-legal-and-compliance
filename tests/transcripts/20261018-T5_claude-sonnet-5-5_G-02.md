## Drafting Notes

**Purpose of the draft:** a balanced, reciprocal first-turn NDA that protects technical and commercial information during partnership evaluation without restricting independent product development.

**Variables to complete:** party names, entity details and addresses; Purpose description; Disclosure Period, survival and return periods; governing law and venue; notice details; signatory authority.

**Negotiable points:**

1. **Definition scope.** Marking is not required in this draft (reasonable-person standard). Counterparties often push for mandatory marking. This is a risk trade-off, and a marking requirement reduces protection for oral disclosures.
2. **Survival period.** The 3-year tail may be challenged (shorter) or extended by you for sensitive technology.
3. **Residuals clause.** Intentionally omitted. Software counterparties often request it. It can materially erode protection for technical know-how. If it is conceded, limit it to unaided memory, exclude source code, trade secrets and personal data, and state that it grants no patent or copyright license.
4. **Section 5.3 (open-source contamination).** May be seen as over-engineered if no code is being exchanged. It can be trimmed.
5. **Representatives, including contractors.** Check this against your internal practice on contractor access.
6. **Fee shifting.** Not included. Add a prevailing-party clause only if your precedent calls for it.
7. **Assignment on change of control.** Allowing it favors acquirers. Consider whether you want consent rights over a competitor acquiring the counterparty.
8. **Not included:** non-solicitation of employees, exclusivity, standstill, data-processing terms, export-control terms and any license for evaluation use of software. Each may be needed depending on the deal.

**Review points before use:**

- Do not send until the Purpose description is accurate. A purpose that is too broad or vague weakens the use restriction.
- Confirm no information will be shared before signature. Pre-signing disclosures may be uncovered.
- If either party is a public company, consider securities-law implications (material non-public information) and whether insider-trading or disclosure provisions are needed.
- If any party is outside the US, or personal data will be shared, additional provisions and review are required.
- If employees of either party will sign individually or the agreement will bind individuals, the whistleblower notice requirement under applicable law should be verified.
- Check against your company's NDA template, signature authority matrix and any contract-management or approval workflow.
