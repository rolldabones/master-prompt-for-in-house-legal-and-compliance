## Short answer: No. Do not run the auto-delete on potentially relevant Slack data.

The fact that no subpoena has arrived does not end the analysis.

- **The duty to preserve generally starts when litigation is reasonably anticipated**, not when a subpoena or complaint arrives. If opposing counsel is already involved, that point may well have passed. *Requires current legal verification; jurisdiction-specific review required.*
- **The stated purpose is the most serious problem.** Deleting data "so there's less to collect later" is the kind of intent courts look at when deciding whether to impose severe spoliation sanctions. Those sanctions can include adverse-inference instructions, monetary penalties or, in serious cases, dismissal or default. Depending on the facts and forum, there may also be obstruction exposure.
- **Routine retention isn't automatically protected.** Ordinary auto-delete schedules are often defensible in normal operations. But once a preservation duty attaches, they generally must be suspended for relevant custodians and channels. Running them anyway, knowingly, is not "normal" anymore.
- **Already-deleted data becomes its own issue.** Gaps can be discovered through other parties' copies, metadata or witness testimony.

## What to do before this weekend

1. **Suspend the scheduled Slack auto-deletion** for potentially relevant custodians, channels and DMs. If you can't scope it reliably by Friday, pause it organization-wide temporarily and narrow it later.
2. **Document the suspension.** Record who instructed it, when, what was paused and who confirmed it in IT.
3. **Check other auto-deletion.** This includes email retention rules, Teams or other messaging, mobile devices and departing-employee wipe processes.
4. **Bring in outside or litigation counsel now.** They can confirm when the duty attached, scope the hold and advise on any data already lost.
5. **Issue a litigation hold** to likely custodians. Track recipients, delivery method, acknowledgments and updates.
6. **Consider insurance notice.** Many policies require prompt notice of a claim or circumstances.
7. **Route further discussion through counsel.** Discussions of preservation strategy should be privilege-protected where possible. Do not create new records that misstate the purpose of retention decisions.

## If the real concern is collection cost

That is a legitimate concern, and it has legitimate solutions:

- narrowing custodians and date ranges
- negotiating proportional discovery scope with opposing counsel
- using targeted search terms and structured Slack exports
- e-discovery vendor pricing

Deletion is not one of the options.
