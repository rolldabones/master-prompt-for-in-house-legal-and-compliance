## User message

## Assistant response

## Mode: LITIGATION RESPONSE (with immediate PRESERVATION action)

Before clarifying questions, there are actions that should be considered now regardless of the answers, because the response deadline and preservation obligations are already running.

---

## A. Do these first (today / this week)

**1. Verify the actual deadline — do not rely on the 21-day assumption.**
The 21-day figure is a common default but is not reliable without checking. Confirm:
- Date and method of service, and on whom (registered agent, officer, substituted service). The clock usually runs from service, not from when Legal received the papers.
- Court and rule set — federal vs. state, and which state. Deadlines and computation rules differ.
- Whether service was waived (waiver can extend the response period substantially).
- Whether the complaint or a summons cover sheet states a different response period.
- Whether local rules or standing orders alter timing.

**Requires current legal verification. Jurisdiction-specific review required.** Docket the earliest defensible date, not the most generous reading, and calendar internal milestones backward from it.

**2. Identify other clocks that may be shorter or parallel.**
Depending on jurisdiction and posture, these may include:
- Removal deadline (often shorter than the answer deadline and easy to blow).
- Deadline for a responsive motion in lieu of an answer.
- Compulsory counterclaim implications — claims not pleaded may be lost.
- Contractual dispute-resolution obligations: arbitration clause, forum selection, mandatory mediation, notice-and-cure provisions. A defense based on these can be waived by litigating.
- Any contractual indemnity or defense tender to a counterparty.

**3. Issue a litigation hold now.**
The preservation obligation likely attached before service — when litigation became reasonably anticipated. Suspend ordinary-course destruction immediately for relevant material.

Scope to define:
- **Custodians:** contract owner, negotiators, signatories, project/delivery team, finance/AR-AP, executives copied on escalations, anyone in the dispute correspondence chain.
- **Date range:** from pre-contract negotiations through the present. Do not cut off at the filing date.
- **Systems:** email, Slack/Teams/chat, shared drives, SharePoint, personal devices used for work, texts, calendars, CRM, contract repository, ERP/billing, ticketing systems, backups, and any auto-delete or ephemeral-messaging settings.
- **Auto-deletion:** confirm with IT that retention/auto-purge rules are suspended for those custodians and channels. This is the most common failure point.
- **Documentation:** record who received the hold, when, how, acknowledgments, and reminders.

No deletion, editing, or "cleaning up" of files. Say this explicitly in the hold.

**4. Notify insurers / consider notice.**
Check for potentially responsive coverage — E&O/professional liability, D&O, CGL, cyber, or a contractual indemnity from a vendor or partner. Late notice is a common, avoidable coverage loss, especially on claims-made policies. If in doubt, give notice; notice is cheap, forfeiture is not.

**5. Impose communication discipline.**
- No unilateral outreach to the plaintiff, its employees, or its counsel by business personnel.
- No internal speculation about liability, fault, or "we probably owe them" in email or chat. Route substantive analysis through counsel and mark appropriately.
- Route all further papers and any plaintiff contact to a single owner in Legal.
- Flag any parallel channels: customer-success emails, account managers, social media, or press inquiries.

**6. Decide on outside counsel now, not at day 15.**
If outside counsel will handle this, engaging early preserves options (removal, motion practice, an extension stipulation). If you need time, an extension of the response deadline is often obtainable by stipulation early — much harder to obtain credibly at the last minute.

## B. What I need from you to go further

**Minimum set:**
1. What court, and which jurisdiction? (Federal/state, and which state.)
2. Date and method of service, and on whom?
3. What is the contract, and who is the plaintiff — customer, vendor, partner, former employee/contractor?
4. Roughly what is claimed, and what is the alleged damages figure or exposure?
5. Are there other causes of action pleaded beyond breach of contract (fraud, misrepresentation, statutory claims)? Those change the risk profile and often the coverage analysis.

**For scoping the response:**
6. Do you have the contract, including any arbitration, forum-selection, limitation-of-liability, notice-and-cure, and indemnity provisions?
7. Was there pre-suit correspondence — demand letters, disputed invoices, termination notices? When did the company first anticipate litigation?
8. Is outside counsel already engaged or identified?
9. Is there a likely counterclaim (unpaid amounts, plaintiff's own breach)?
10. Who is the internal decision owner, and does this need executive or board visibility?
11. Any insurance policies you want me to help map notice obligations against?

Tell me what you have and I can produce, in order: a litigation hold notice draft, a custodian/systems scoping matrix, an immediate-action checklist with dated milestones, a chronology template, and an outside-counsel scoping/instruction package.

## C. Escalation

This is an escalation matter now, not a routine one. At minimum:
- **General Counsel / senior legal owner:** immediately.
- **CFO / finance:** if the claimed amount is potentially material for accrual, disclosure, or audit purposes. Loss-contingency accounting questions can arise quickly.
- **IT:** same-day, for auto-deletion suspension.
- **Business owner of the relationship:** with clear instructions on communication discipline.
- **Executive or board:** if the amount, reputational exposure, or counterparty relationship is material.
