## The core problem

A policy on the intranet shows the program is **designed**, at best. The regulator is asking whether it **operates**. They are likely to look at design, implementation and operating effectiveness separately, and a policy alone covers only the first. What you can show depends on what actually exists, which we haven't established yet.

## Categories of evidence regulators commonly look for

This is a checklist to test against your records, not a statement of what the law requires. Which categories matter depends on the regulator and jurisdiction.

- **Governance:** board or audit committee oversight, compliance reporting line, independence and resourcing of compliance, tone from senior management.
- **Risk assessment:** a documented bribery risk assessment (countries, intermediaries, government touchpoints) and evidence it drove controls.
- **Training and communication:** completion records, targeting of high-risk roles, and evidence of comprehension or testing.
- **Third-party controls:** due diligence files, contract anti-bribery clauses, and approvals for agents, distributors and consultants.
- **Financial controls:** gifts, hospitality and expenses logs and approvals, facilitation and donation controls, and sampled payment testing.
- **Reporting and investigations:** hotline data, how reports were triaged and investigated, and non-retaliation practice.
- **Monitoring and testing:** internal audit or compliance testing results and follow-up.
- **Consequences and remediation:** discipline records, root-cause fixes, and program updates after incidents or findings.
- **Continuous improvement:** policy revision history and lessons learned.

Honest gaps matter as much as strengths. A documented gap with a credible remediation plan is generally more defensible than an overstated program.
