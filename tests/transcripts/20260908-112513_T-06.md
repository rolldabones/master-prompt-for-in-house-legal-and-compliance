# T-06 Compliance Program

**Model:** claude-opus-5  **Run:** 20260908-112513

## User message

A regulator asked for evidence that our anti-bribery compliance program actually works. We have a policy on the intranet. What do we show them?

## Assistant response

---

## 1. Immediate flags

**This is an escalation matter.** A regulator asking for evidence of anti-bribery program effectiveness is not a routine information request. It should be run by, or at minimum reviewed by, the General Counsel and experienced ABC/investigations counsel before anything goes out the door.

**Preservation action should be considered now.** A regulator inquiry is a preservation trigger. Suspend routine destruction across the systems likely in scope: compliance case management, hotline, training LMS, third-party due diligence platform, ERP/payment and expense systems, email and messaging for relevant custodians, board and committee materials.

**Do not create, backdate, reconstruct or "tidy up" records to fill gaps.** Producing documents that imply they existed contemporaneously when they did not is a separate and far worse problem than a weak program. Anything generated now to describe the program must be clearly dated and presented as prepared in response to the request.

**Do not overstate.** Misstatements to a regulator about program operation create independent exposure. The response should be accurate about what exists, including what does not.

**A policy on the intranet is not evidence that a program works.** Regulators distinguish three things and will ask about all three:

| | What it means | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Design** | Is the program well designed for this company's risk? | Risk assessment, policy suite, controls architecture |
| **Implementation** | Is it resourced and applied in good faith? | Headcount, budget, reporting line, training delivery, system deployment |
| **Operating effectiveness** | Does it actually work in practice? | Testing results, hotline and investigation outcomes, discipline, third parties rejected, controls that stopped something |

Most companies can evidence design. Far fewer can evidence operating effectiveness. That is where the request is aimed.

## 2. Questions I need before producing anything tailored

1. Which regulator, and in which jurisdiction? **Jurisdiction-specific review required.**
2. Is the request formal (subpoena, statutory notice, CID) or informal? Please provide the exact wording and any stated legal basis.
3. What is the deadline?
4. Is this a thematic/sector review, a licensing or supervisory review, or is it connected to a specific allegation, transaction, counterparty or country?
5. Is outside counsel already engaged? Is the matter being run under privilege?
6. Which legal entities, countries and business lines are in scope?
7. Are there any existing undertakings, prior findings, DPAs, monitorships or certifications that constrain the response?
8. Who owns the response internally, and who approves what goes out?
9. Do you already know of any conduct issue, open investigation or hotline matter touching bribery risk?

Question 9 matters most. If the answer is yes, the sequencing changes entirely and this becomes an investigation matter before it is a production matter.

## 3. Provisional evidence map

Use this to scope what you actually hold. For each line, the test is: **dated, system-generated or contemporaneous, attributable, and capable of being sampled by a third party.**

**A. Governance and tone**
- Board or audit committee minutes and packs showing ABC on the agenda, with substantive discussion — not a standing item with no content
- Compliance function reporting line, direct access to the board/committee, executive sessions
- Compliance headcount, budget and seniority over time; evidence of autonomy
- Senior leadership communications on ABC (dated, attributable, not generic)

**B. Risk assessment**
- Methodology, inputs, country/third-party/transaction risk ranking, refresh cadence
- Critically: documented traceability showing that assessment findings changed something — a control, a policy, training targeting, a market exit

**C. Policies and procedures**
- Version history, approval records, effective dates, translations
- Distribution and acknowledgment records with completion rates by population
- Underlying procedures: gifts/hospitality/travel, charitable and political contributions, facilitation payments, sponsorships, use of intermediaries

**D. Training and communication**
- Completion data by business unit and risk tier, not just a global percentage
- Targeted, role-specific training for high-risk roles (sales, procurement, government-facing, JV staff)
- Assessment scores, refresher cadence, content samples, evidence of follow-up on non-completers

**E. Third parties and intermediaries**
- Risk-ranked due diligence files, including refresh cycles
- Contract terms: ABC representations, audit rights, termination rights, no-subcontracting without consent
- **Examples of red flags escalated, of onboarding refused, and of relationships terminated.** This is one of the highest-value evidence categories.
- Ongoing monitoring, certifications, sanctions/PEP screening

**F. Financial controls and books and records**
- Payment approval matrix, vendor onboarding controls, segregation of duties
- Expense review and exception reporting, petty cash and cash controls in high-risk locations
- Evidence of controls detecting or blocking something — rejected payments, flagged expenses, corrected coding

**G. Reporting channels**
- Hotline availability, languages, anonymity, third-party operation
- Non-retaliation policy plus evidence it is applied
- Volume, category and closure-time metrics over several years; trend analysis and what was done with it

**H. Investigations and discipline**
- Investigation protocol; case files showing intake, scoping, method, findings, closure
- Root cause analysis, not just outcome
- **Discipline records showing consistency — including for senior or revenue-generating employees.** Regulators specifically test whether the rules apply upward.
- Remediation actions traceable to specific investigation findings

**I. Incentives**
- Compliance objectives in performance reviews and compensation; clawback provisions; certifications by managers

**J. Monitoring, testing and audit**
- Internal audit plan coverage of ABC risk; audit reports and findings
- Transaction testing, data analytics, in-country spot checks
- Remediation tracking to closure with dates

**K. M&A**
- Pre-acquisition ABC due diligence and post-closing integration of controls

**L. Continuous improvement**
- External reviews or benchmarking; documented program changes with the reason for each change

## 4. How to present it

Regulators generally respond better to a structured narrative supported by sampled evidence than to a document dump.

Suggested structure, subject to counsel:
1. Program overview and governance
2. Risk assessment and how it drives the program
3. Controls by risk area, with the evidence of operation for each
4. Detection, reporting, investigation and discipline, with anonymized case metrics
5. Testing and assurance results
6. Known gaps and the remediation plan, with owners and dates

Point 6 is uncomfortable but usually the right call. A candid, prioritized remediation plan with named owners and dates is generally viewed more favorably than a polished account that a regulator's own testing then contradicts. **Confirm this approach with outside counsel before adopting it** — it is a judgment call that depends on the regulator, the legal basis of the request and whether misconduct is suspected.

---

## 5. Privilege and process cautions

- Any internal gap assessment you run now should be scoped under counsel direction with privilege considered from the outset. **Privilege positioning is jurisdiction-specific and cannot be assumed.**
- Decide deliberately what is produced versus what is privileged. Production of privileged assessments can carry waiver consequences beyond this request.
- Keep a production log: what was requested, what was searched, what was produced, when, and by whom.
- Circulate the response draft on a need-to-know basis.
- Confirm the environment you are using is approved for regulator-facing and potentially privileged material before pasting documents or case detail into it.

---

## 6. Recommended first moves this week

1. Notify GC; confirm outside counsel engagement and who directs the response.
2. Issue or update preservation instructions across the systems listed above; record recipients and acknowledgments.
3. Clarify the request's scope, legal basis and deadline with the regulator in writing if ambiguous — usually via counsel.
4. Run a rapid internal inventory against the evidence map: exists / partially exists / does not exist. Do not remediate by document creation.
5. Assess whether any known conduct issue exists that changes the character of this matter.
6. Consider whether insurance notice (D&O, regulatory defence costs) may be required — check policy notification triggers now, as they are often early and strict.
