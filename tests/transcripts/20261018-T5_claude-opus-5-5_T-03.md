## Before You Send: Points That Affect the Draft

1. **Do not describe the email as "extortion" or a "threat," inside or outside the notice.** A former employee raising safety concerns may be engaged in legally protected whistleblower activity even if they paired it with a severance demand. Characterizing it negatively in a discoverable or semi-discoverable document creates retaliation and credibility risk. The draft below uses neutral language. **Jurisdiction-specific review required.**
2. **Preservation must not delay safety remediation.** If a hazard exists, fix it. Before repair, alteration or disposal of equipment, document its condition with photos, video and written records. The notice says this explicitly.
3. **The departed employee's data is at the highest risk.** IT routinely wipes, reimages or reassigns laptops and phones, and deletes mailboxes on a schedule. A separate IT instruction (Part B) should go out the same day as the notice, ideally before it.
4. **The underlying safety allegation is its own escalation track.** It is not just a litigation risk. It may involve regulatory reporting obligations, depending on jurisdiction and facts. **Requires current legal verification.**

> **1. Why you are receiving this notice**
>
> [Company] anticipates a potential legal dispute relating to workplace safety matters at [Plant Name/Location] and to the employment and separation of a former employee. You are receiving this notice because you may have documents or information relevant to this matter.
>
> This notice is a legal obligation. It overrides any normal document retention schedule, auto-delete setting or routine practice that would otherwise result in the deletion, alteration or disposal of relevant information. It does not suggest that you have done anything wrong.
>
> **2. What you must preserve**
>
> Effective immediately, preserve all documents, data and physical items, in any form, that relate to:
>
> - Safety conditions, incidents, near-misses, hazards, inspections, audits or complaints at [Plant Name] from [START DATE] to the present;

> - Communications received from [Former Employee Name] after separation, and any internal communications about them;
> - [Any additional topics specific to the matter].
>
> **"Documents and data" includes, without limitation:**
> - Email (including personal email accounts, if used for work)
> - Text messages, iMessage, WhatsApp, Signal, Teams, Slack and other messaging apps, on company *or personal* devices if used for work
> - Voicemail and call logs
> - Word documents, spreadsheets, presentations and PDFs
> - Calendars and meeting notes
> - Files on shared drives, SharePoint, OneDrive, Google Drive, Box or similar

> - Safety management systems, maintenance/CMMS systems, incident reporting systems, HR systems and timekeeping records

> - Handwritten notes, logbooks, binders, whiteboard photos and paper files

>

## Questions to Finalize the Draft

1. **Jurisdiction(s):** Which country and state is the plant in, and where did the former employee work?
2. **Date range:** When did the employee's tenure start? When were the earliest safety concerns at issue? This sets [START DATE].
3. **Custodians:** Who should receive the notice? Likely candidates are the CEO, the employee's former manager and chain, the plant manager, EHS/safety lead, maintenance lead, HR business partner, whoever negotiated the severance, IT, and anyone the CEO forwarded the email to.
4. **The email itself:** What specifically did it allege, and has anyone responded? Any reply should go through counsel.
5. **Prior complaints:** Did the employee raise safety concerns internally before or during separation? This bears directly on retaliation exposure.
6. **Severance agreement:** Was a release signed? What confidentiality, non-disparagement and whistleblower carve-out terms does it include?
7. **Outside counsel:** Are they engaged? Should the notice issue from them to strengthen privilege positioning?
8. **Platform:** Do you use a legal hold tool, or will this go by email?
