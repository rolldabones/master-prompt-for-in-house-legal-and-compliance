## What matters right now

**1. A policy on the intranet is not evidence that a program works.** Regulators typically test three things:
- whether the program is well designed,
- whether it is resourced and empowered,
- whether it works in practice.

A policy addresses only part of the first. What persuades a regulator is evidence of operation over time: records showing the program was used, tested, enforced and improved. The specific standard depends on the regulator and jurisdiction. **Requires current legal verification. Jurisdiction-specific review required.**

**2. Do not manufacture history.**
- Do not backdate, retroactively "complete," or reformat documents to imply controls existed when they did not.
- If gaps exist, the defensible position is to describe the current state accurately and present remediation as remediation.
- Overstating your program to a regulator can create risk far larger than the gap itself.

**3. Escalation is triggered now.**
- **Why:** a regulator inquiry touching anti-bribery may signal an underlying allegation or sector sweep, and your response becomes part of the regulatory record.
- **To whom:** General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer. Probably also the Audit Committee chair, depending on the context of the request.
- **Urgency:** high. Confirm any response deadline today.
- **Outside counsel:** **Escalate to specialist or outside counsel** with anti-corruption regulatory experience, particularly if the request is formal or connected to a specific matter.

**4. Preservation action should be considered now.** The scope depends on whether the request relates to a specific transaction, region or person.

**5. Insurance.** Check whether D&O or other policies require notice of regulatory inquiries. Notice windows can be short.

**6. Privilege.** Any internal gap assessment should be structured under the direction of counsel. Circulate on a need-to-know basis, and avoid casual written characterizations such as "we have no real program."

## What evidence to look for

Use this as an inventory checklist to find what actually exists. It is not a list of things to create.

| Area | Evidence of operation |
|---|---|
| Governance and oversight | Board or audit committee minutes discussing anti-bribery; compliance reports to the board; reporting line and access of the compliance lead |
| Risk assessment | Documented bribery risk assessment, update history, and how it shaped controls |
| Policies and procedures | Version history; gifts, hospitality and charitable/political giving procedures; approval records; employee certifications |
| Training | Completion data; role-targeted training for high-risk staff; content; testing results |
| Third parties | Due-diligence files, red-flag resolution, contractual anti-bribery clauses, audit rights actually exercised |
| Financial controls | Payment approvals, gifts and hospitality registers, expense review, monitoring or analytics outputs |
| Speak-up channels | Hotline volumes, case-handling timelines, anti-retaliation measures |
| Investigations and discipline | Case outcomes, consistency of discipline, root-cause analysis |
| Testing and audit | Internal audit reports on anti-bribery, findings, remediation tracking |
| Resourcing | Compliance headcount, budget, independence |
| Continuous improvement | Program changes made in response to incidents, audits or risk changes |
| Incentives | Compliance considerations in compensation or promotion, if any |
