## B. DRAFT — MUTUAL NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT

> **MUTUAL NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT**

This Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of [EFFECTIVE DATE] (the "**Effective Date**") by and between:

**[COMPANY LEGAL NAME]**, a [STATE] [ENTITY TYPE], with its principal place of business at [ADDRESS] ("**[COMPANY SHORT NAME]**"); and

**[COUNTERPARTY LEGAL NAME]**, a [STATE/COUNTRY] [ENTITY TYPE], with its principal place of business at [ADDRESS] ("**[COUNTERPARTY SHORT NAME]**").

Each of the foregoing is a "**Party**" and together they are the "**Parties**."

**RECITALS**

The Parties wish to explore a potential business relationship involving [DESCRIBE: e.g., integration of the Parties' respective software products, a technology partnership, a reseller or OEM arrangement] (the "**Purpose**"). In connection with the Purpose, each Party may disclose to the other certain confidential and proprietary information. The Parties enter into this Agreement to protect such information.

**1. Definitions**

**1.1 "Confidential Information"** means any non-public information disclosed or made available by or on behalf of a Party (the "**Disclosing Party**") to the other Party (the "**Receiving Party**") in connection with the Purpose, whether disclosed orally, visually, in writing, electronically or in any other form, and whether or not marked or identified as confidential, that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential or proprietary given its nature or the circumstances of disclosure. Confidential Information includes, without limitation:

(a) software, source code, object code, algorithms, data models, data structures, APIs, technical specifications, architecture and system design, security architecture, and development, testing and deployment methodologies;

(b) product plans, product roadmaps, release schedules, unreleased features, and research and development activities;

(c) business plans, financial information, pricing, cost structures, margins, forecasts, and unannounced business strategies;

(d) customer and prospect lists, customer requirements, supplier and vendor information, and terms of third-party agreements;

(e) personnel information, organizational structure and compensation information;

(f) results of security assessments, penetration tests, audits, vulnerability disclosures and incident information;

(g) the existence and content of the discussions between the Parties regarding the Purpose, and the existence and terms of this Agreement; and

(h) any notes, analyses, summaries, compilations, models, studies or other materials prepared by the Receiving Party or its Representatives to the extent they contain, reflect or are derived from any of the foregoing ("**Derived Materials**").

**1.2 "Representatives"** means, with respect to a Party, its directors, officers, employees, and its legal, financial and technical advisors, in each case (a) who have a bona fide need to know the Confidential Information for the Purpose and (b) who are bound by written or professional obligations of confidentiality and restricted use no less protective than those in this Agreement. [OPTIONAL: "Representatives" also includes Affiliates of a Party, and the Parties' respective contractors and subcontractors meeting the foregoing conditions.]

**1.3 "Affiliate"** means any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with a Party, where "control" means ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities or equivalent voting interest.

**2. Exclusions**

Confidential Information does not include information that the Receiving Party can demonstrate by contemporaneous written records:

(a) was lawfully in the Receiving Party's possession without obligation of confidentiality prior to disclosure by the Disclosing Party;

(b) is or becomes publicly available through no act or omission of the Receiving Party or its Representatives in breach of this Agreement;

(c) is lawfully received by the Receiving Party from a third party without obligation of confidentiality and without breach of any duty owed to the Disclosing Party; or

(d) is independently developed by the Receiving Party or its Representatives without use of or reference to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information.

**3. Obligations of the Receiving Party**

**3.1 Use Restriction.** The Receiving Party shall use the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information solely for the Purpose and for no other purpose. Without limiting the foregoing, the Receiving Party shall not use the Confidential Information to develop, enhance or market any product or service, or for competitive analysis or benchmarking against the Disclosing Party.

**3.2 Non-Disclosure.** The Receiving Party shall not disclose the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information to any person other than its Representatives. The Receiving Party shall be responsible for any act or omission of its Representatives that would constitute a breach of this Agreement if committed by the Receiving Party.

**3.3 Standard of Care.** The Receiving Party shall protect the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information using at least the same degree of care it uses to protect its own confidential information of similar importance, and in no event less than a reasonable degree of care. The Receiving Party shall maintain administrative, technical and physical safeguards reasonably designed to protect the Confidential Information against unauthorized access, use, disclosure, alteration or destruction.

**3.4 No Reverse Engineering.** The Receiving Party shall not, and shall not permit any third party to, reverse engineer, decompile, disassemble or otherwise attempt to derive source code, structure, sequence, organization or trade secrets from any software, code, prototype, sample, demonstration environment or other tangible or intangible embodiment of Confidential Information made available by the Disclosing Party, except to the extent this restriction is unenforceable under applicable law.

**3.5 Notice of Unauthorized Disclosure.** The Receiving Party shall promptly, and in any event within [FIVE (5)] business days of becoming aware, notify the Disclosing Party in writing of any unauthorized use, disclosure, loss or compromise of Confidential Information, shall cooperate reasonably with the Disclosing Party, and shall take reasonable steps to mitigate and prevent recurrence.

**3.6 No Circumvention of Systems.** Where a Party is provided access to the other Party's systems, environments or credentials for evaluation purposes, such access shall be used only as authorized and only for the Purpose, and shall be subject to any access policies communicated by the granting Party.

**4. Compelled Disclosure**

If the Receiving Party or any of its Representatives is required by applicable law, regulation, subpoena, court order, or the rules of any securities exchange or governmental or regulatory authority to disclose any Confidential Information, the Receiving Party shall, to the extent legally permitted and reasonably practicable, provide prompt written notice to the Disclosing Party so that the Disclosing Party may seek a protective order or other remedy, and shall reasonably cooperate with the Disclosing Party's efforts (at the Disclosing Party's expense). If such protection is not obtained, the Receiving Party may disclose only that portion of the Confidential Information it is legally required to disclose and shall use reasonable efforts to obtain confidential treatment for it. Disclosure made in accordance with this Section 4 shall not constitute a breach of this Agreement.

**5. No License; Ownership**

All Confidential Information remains the property of the Disclosing Party. Nothing in this Agreement grants the Receiving Party any license or right, by implication, estoppel or otherwise, under any patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret or other intellectual property right of the Disclosing Party, other than the limited right to use the Confidential Information for the Purpose in accordance with this Agreement. No Party is obligated to disclose any particular information.

**6. Feedback**

If a Receiving Party provides suggestions, comments or feedback regarding the Disclosing Party's products, services or technology ("**Feedback**"), the Disclosing Party may use such Feedback without restriction or obligation, provided that the Disclosing Party shall not identify the Receiving Party as the source and such use does not require disclosure of the Receiving Party's Confidential Information. [NEGOTIABLE — see review notes.]

**7. Term; Survival**

**7.1** This Agreement commences on the Effective Date and continues for [TWO (2)] years, unless earlier terminated by either Party on [THIRTY (30)] days' written notice (the "**Term**"). Termination does not affect obligations with respect to Confidential Information disclosed before termination.

**7.2** The obligations of confidentiality and restricted use in this Agreement survive for [THREE (3)] years from the date of disclosure of the applicable Confidential Information; provided that Confidential Information constituting a trade secret under applicable law shall be protected for as long as it remains a trade secret.

**8. Return or Destruction**

Upon the Disclosing Party's written request, or upon expiration or termination of the Term, the Receiving Party shall, at the Disclosing Party's election, promptly return or destroy all Confidential Information of the Disclosing Party in its or its Representatives' possession, including Derived Materials, and upon request certify such return or destruction in writing signed by an authorized officer. The Receiving Party may retain (a) copies required to be retained by applicable law, regulation or bona fide internal document-retention or compliance policy, and (b) copies contained in routine, non-targeted electronic backups, in each case subject to the continuing confidentiality and use obligations of this Agreement for so long as such copies are retained, notwithstanding any expiration under Section 7.2.

**9. No Obligation; No Exclusivity**

This Agreement does not obligate either Party to proceed with any transaction, to enter into any further agreement, or to negotiate on any particular terms. Each Party may terminate discussions at any time for any reason. Nothing in this Agreement restricts either Party from independently developing, acquiring, marketing or providing any products or services, or from entering into any relationship with any third party, including a competitor of the other Party, provided it does so without breach of this Agreement.

**10. No Warranty**

ALL CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION IS PROVIDED "AS IS." NEITHER PARTY MAKES ANY REPRESENTATION OR WARRANTY, EXPRESS OR IMPLIED, AS TO THE ACCURACY, COMPLETENESS, NON-INFRINGEMENT, MERCHANTABILITY OR FITNESS FOR A PARTICULAR PURPOSE OF ANY CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION. Neither Party shall have any liability to the other resulting from the other Party's reliance on Confidential Information, except as expressly provided in a definitive written agreement subsequently executed by the Parties.

**11. Remedies**

Each Party acknowledges that unauthorized use or disclosure of Confidential Information may cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages would be an inadequate remedy. Accordingly, the Disclosing Party is entitled to seek injunctive or other equitable relief without the necessity of posting a bond, in addition to all other remedies available at law or in equity. [Consider deleting "without the necessity of posting a bond" if enforceability in the chosen forum is uncertain.]

**12. Personal Data**

The Parties do not intend to exchange personal data in connection with the Purpose. If either Party determines that personal data must be disclosed, the Parties shall first agree in writing on appropriate terms, including any required data processing or transfer terms, before such disclosure occurs. [Replace with a full data protection annex if personal data will in fact be exchanged.]

**13. Export Control and Trade Compliance**

Each Party shall comply with all applicable export control, sanctions and trade laws in connection with its receipt, use and disclosure of Confidential Information, and shall not export, re-export or transfer any Confidential Information in violation of such laws.

**14. Notices**

All notices under this Agreement shall be in writing and delivered by personal delivery, nationally recognized overnight courier, or email with confirmation of transmission, to the addresses set out below the Parties' signatures (or as updated by notice). Notices are effective upon receipt.

**15. Miscellaneous**

**15.1 Governing Law; Venue.** This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of [DELAWARE], without regard to its conflict-of-laws principles. The Parties submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in [COUNTY, STATE], and waive any objection to venue or forum non conveniens. **[VERIFY — see review notes.]**

**15.2 Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the other Party's prior written consent, except that either Party may assign it in its entirety to a successor in connection with a merger, reorganization or sale of all or substantially all of its assets or the business to which this Agreement relates, on written notice to the other Party. [Consider adding: consent required where the assignee is a competitor of the non-assigning Party.] Any purported assignment in violation of this Section is void.

**15.3 Entire Agreement; Amendment.** This Agreement is the entire agreement between the Parties regarding its subject matter and supersedes all prior or contemporaneous understandings on that subject. It may be amended only by a written instrument signed by both Parties.

**15.4 No Waiver.** No failure or delay in exercising any right operates as a waiver, and no single or partial exercise precludes any further exercise.

**15.5 Severability.** If any provision is held unenforceable, it shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable, and the remaining provisions remain in full force.

**15.6 Relationship of the Parties.** The Parties are independent contractors. This Agreement creates no partnership, joint venture, agency or employment relationship.

**15.7 Counterparts; Electronic Signature.** This Agreement may be executed in counterparts, including by electronic signature, each of which is an original and all of which together constitute one instrument.

## D. NEGOTIABLE POINTS (expect counterparty pushback here)

| Provision | Likely counterparty position | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **§1.1 unmarked information protected** | Push to require written marking, or oral disclosure confirmed in writing within 30 days | Marking regimes are administratively burdensome and frequently fail in practice. Resist if you will be a significant discloser. |
| **§1.1(h) Derived Materials** | Push to exclude internal analyses from return/destruction | Reasonable middle ground: Derived Materials remain confidential but may be destroyed rather than returned. |
| **§3.1 no competitive benchmarking** | May be resisted by a party with a competing product | Consider carving out independently developed products. |
| **§3.4 no reverse engineering** | Fine if no code changes hands; contested if evaluation involves binaries | Keep. |
| **§6 Feedback** | Some counterparties strike entirely or make it non-exclusive license only | Low value in a pure evaluation NDA. Droppable as a trade. |
| **§7.2 three-year tail + perpetual trade secrets** | Counterparties often want a flat term with no trade secret carve-out | The trade secret carve-out is worth defending — a flat term can be read to extinguish trade secret protection. |
| **Residuals clause** | Counterparty may propose one ("unaided memory" of Representatives) | **Not included by default, deliberately.** A residuals clause substantially weakens protection for software architecture and roadmap information. Escalate to the business owner before accepting one. |
| **§9 no exclusivity / independent development** | Usually accepted | Keep. Protects you against later claims. |
| **§15.1 governing law** | Standard forum fight | See review notes. |
| **Non-solicit of employees** | Counterparty may request | Not included. Enforceability is highly jurisdiction-dependent, and it is a separate commercial decision. **Jurisdiction-specific review required** if added. |

## E. REVIEW POINTS BEFORE USE

1. **Confirm the assumptions in Section A.** Several would materially change the draft:
   - If **personal data** will be exchanged (e.g., customer records in a demo environment), Section 12 is insufficient — a data processing addendum is needed. **Jurisdiction-specific review required.**
   - If the counterparty is **non-U.S.**, revisit governing law, venue, data transfer, export control and enforceability of injunctive relief.
   - If either party is **publicly traded** or the discussions could involve material non-public information, consider MNPI/insider trading acknowledgments and whether a standstill is appropriate.
   - If **source code will actually be delivered** rather than demonstrated, an NDA is not enough — a code escrow/evaluation license with defined scope is the right instrument.
2. **Governing law and venue (§15.1).** Delaware is a placeholder only. Choose based on where you can practically enforce and where injunctive relief is realistically obtainable. **Requires current legal verification.**
3. **Check against your internal NDA form and approval matrix.** If a company standard NDA exists, use it as the base instead; this draft is more useful as a comparison checklist. **Unknown/Insufficient data** — I do not have your form or signature authority rules.
4. **Signature authority.** Confirm the intended signatory has authority to bind the entity under your delegation of authority.
5. **Entity check.** Confirm the counterparty's exact legal name and whether Affiliates need to be bound. If the deal will actually be performed by a subsidiary, name the correct entity or add an Affiliate provision.
6. **Operational reality.** An NDA only works if people follow it. Before disclosure begins, confirm: who on your side is authorized to disclose; what is in scope; whether roadmap or unreleased-feature information requires separate approval; and how received counterparty materials will be stored and segregated.
7. **Do not rely on this draft as legal advice.** It is a starting document for review by qualified counsel.

## Ending Block

**Provided facts:** Potential software partnership; mutual NDA requested; instruction to proceed on standard assumptions without clarifying questions.

**Assumptions:** As listed in Section A (nine assumptions), plus placeholder Delaware governing law and 2-year term / 3-year confidentiality tail.

**Unknown/Insufficient data:** Party identities and entity types; jurisdictions; whether a company standard NDA form exists; scope and nature of information to be exchanged; whether source code, personal data or MNPI is involved; signature authority; whether Affiliates or contractors need coverage; deadline; whether outside counsel is involved.

**Key risks:**
- Purpose clause left blank — the use restriction in §3.1 is only as strong as the Purpose definition.
- If personal data or actual source code will change hands, this instrument is inadequate as drafted.
- Governing law is a placeholder; enforceability of injunctive relief and any later-added restrictive covenants is jurisdiction-dependent.
- Accepting a counterparty residuals clause would materially weaken protection for architecture and roadmap information — this is the single most consequential negotiation point in a software-partnership NDA.
- Signing an NDA without disclosure discipline on the business side creates false comfort.

**Escalation triggers:** Counterparty proposes a residuals clause, a standstill, exclusivity or a non-solicit; counterparty is non-U.S.; personal data or regulated data enters scope; source code delivery is contemplated; counterparty is a direct competitor; either party is public and MNPI may be exchanged.

**Documents/data to gather next:** Company standard NDA form and fallback positions; delegation of authority / signature matrix; counterparty entity details; description of information intended to be disclosed; any existing agreement between the parties that may already cover confidentiality.

**Recommended next steps:** (1) Confirm or correct the nine assumptions; (2) draft the Purpose clause; (3) compare against the company standard form; (4) select governing law and venue; (5) route for signature authority confirmation; (6) brief the business team on disclosure scope before any information is exchanged.

**Needs specialist or outside counsel review:** Yes if any of the following apply — non-U.S. counterparty, personal data in scope, source code delivery, MNPI/securities dimension, or addition of restrictive covenants.
