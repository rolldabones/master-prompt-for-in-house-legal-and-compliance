## A. Do in the next 24–72 hours

**1. Confirm the real deadline. Do not rely on "21 days."**

The response deadline depends on court, rule set, service method, party status and any local standing orders. Twenty-one days is the federal default under FRCP 12(a) for a defendant served with process, but state courts vary widely, waiver-of-service and out-of-state service change the count, and the trigger date depends on when and how service was effected — which may not be yesterday.

- Pull the actual summons and confirm what it says on its face.
- Confirm date, method and recipient of service. Was the registered agent served? Was it hand-delivered to a receptionist? Was it mailed?
- **Requires current legal verification / Jurisdiction-specific review required.** Do not let a business stakeholder calendar this from memory.

**2. Check the other clocks that are already running.**

An answer deadline is usually not the only one, and some are shorter or non-extendable:

- **Removal**, if this is state court and federal jurisdiction may exist — typically 30 days from service in federal practice, and it is a hard deadline. Requires current legal verification.
- **Arbitration or forum-selection provisions** in the underlying contract. If the contract has an arbitration clause or a mandatory forum, filing an answer without addressing it can waive or complicate the position.
- **Contractual notice-of-claim or indemnity notice deadlines** running to insurers, customers, suppliers or upstream parties.
- **Insurance notice** — see item 5.
- Any TRO, preliminary injunction, expedited discovery or order to show cause bundled with the complaint. Check the served packet page by page; these carry much shorter deadlines.
- Local rules on corporate disclosure statements, initial appearances, scheduling conferences.

**3. Issue a litigation hold today.**

Do not wait for outside counsel to be retained. A hold issued imperfectly today is better than a perfect hold in two weeks.

- **Custodians:** contract owner and their manager; the negotiator(s) of the contract; account/relationship manager; project or delivery leads; finance/AR or AP personnel who handled invoicing and payment; anyone in the plaintiff communication chain; the executive sponsor. Add IT as a recipient for systems action, not as a substantive custodian.
- **Date span:** from pre-contract negotiation through today, with no end date. Do not cut it off at the alleged breach.
- **Systems:** email; Teams/Slack/chat; personal devices used for work; shared drives and SharePoint; CRM; contract repository; ERP/accounting and invoicing; ticketing or project management tools; calendars; voicemail; text messages; any WhatsApp or SMS threads with the counterparty; backups; departed-employee mailboxes.
- **Critical:** suspend auto-delete and ephemeral-message settings on messaging platforms and mailboxes, and suspend routine destruction under the records retention schedule for in-scope material. This is a separate IT action from sending the hold notice. It is where most spoliation problems originate.
- **Document the hold itself:** who received it, when, how, acknowledgments received, follow-ups, and updates. The hold process will itself be discoverable.
- Never delete, alter or "clean up" anything. Tell custodians this in plain terms.

I can draft the hold notice and the custodian acknowledgment tracker as soon as you confirm the fact basics.

**4. Impose communication discipline immediately.**

- No one contacts the plaintiff, its counsel or its employees about the dispute except through designated counsel.
- No internal "what happened here" email threads speculating on fault, damages or who dropped the ball. Those threads are discoverable and are typically the worst documents in the case. Fact-gathering should be run through counsel with a privilege header and need-to-know circulation.
- No public, customer-facing or social commentary.
- Circulate the complaint on a need-to-know basis only.

**5. Evaluate insurance notice now.**

Pure breach-of-contract claims are frequently excluded, but that is not a reason to skip notice, and the complaint may plead tort, misrepresentation, IP or negligence counts alongside the contract count. Consider: E&O/professional liability, D&O, cyber, tech/media, CGL, and any contractual indemnity from a subcontractor or vendor. Policies commonly require prompt notice, and late notice is a common coverage-forfeiture trap. Give notice under any policy that plausibly responds and let the carrier decline in writing.

**6. Retain or task counsel this week, not next.**

Twenty-one days is not a long runway once you account for conflicts checks, engagement, document review and drafting. If the deadline is real, an extension is usually obtainable by stipulation, but that request should come from counsel and should be made early, not on day 19. Note that in some courts a stipulated extension requires court approval and can affect removal or arbitration positioning — **jurisdiction-specific review required.**

**7. Assign an internal owner today.**

One named person accountable for the deadline calendar, the hold, the document collection and the counsel relationship. "Nobody has done anything yet" is a governance failure, not a legal one, and it recurs unless ownership is explicit.

## B. What I need from you to go further

**On the filing:**
1. What court — state or federal, and which state/district? What is the caption and case number?
2. When and how were you served, and on whom? Do you have the summons and proof/affidavit of service?
3. Was anything filed *with* the complaint — TRO/injunction application, expedited discovery, lis pendens, motion?
4. Who is the plaintiff, and are there co-defendants (including any of your employees or affiliates)?

**On the substance:**
5. What contract is at issue? Do you have the executed version with all amendments, SOWs and exhibits? Does it contain an arbitration clause, forum selection, choice of law, limitation of liability, notice-and-cure provision, or fee-shifting clause?
6. What is the alleged breach and the damages claimed?
7. Was there a prior demand letter, notice of default, cure notice or settlement discussion? If so, when — and was it escalated internally at the time?
8. Do you have potential counterclaims or third-party claims (e.g., against a subcontractor)?

**On posture:**
9. Is outside counsel already engaged or identified? Who is the internal decision-owner and what is the approval path for retention and settlement authority?
10. Who inside the company currently knows about this? Has anyone communicated with the plaintiff since service?
11. Is this material enough to require board, audit committee, auditor or disclosure consideration (accrual/contingency, loss reserve, financial-statement footnote, or any securities disclosure obligation)?
12. Is your records retention system capable of a legal hold, and who administers it?

## Ending Block

**Assumptions:** Service was validly effected on the company; the "21 days" figure derives from the summons or the applicable rule; no injunctive or expedited relief was sought in the same filing; no litigation hold is currently in place. All are unconfirmed.

**Unknown/Insufficient data:** Court and jurisdiction; exact service date, method and recipient; contract terms including arbitration, forum, choice of law and limitation of liability; nature and amount of alleged damages; prior demand or cure correspondence; existence of counterclaims; insurance program; internal ownership and approval path; whether outside counsel is engaged; whether any responsive communications have already occurred.

**Key risks:**

- **Waiver.** Filing an answer without first addressing arbitration, forum selection, jurisdiction or removal can forfeit those positions permanently.
- **Coverage forfeiture** through late notice.
- **Bad-document creation** through uncontrolled internal email speculation.
- **Missed removal window**, which is short and unforgiving.

**Escalation triggers:** Pending litigation (triggered). Likely litigation hold trigger (triggered). Possible insurance notice
